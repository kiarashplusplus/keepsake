# Campus Geo-Scoping Research: Precedents, Abuse Patterns, and Houston Geography

## Verdict

Geography-only campus scoping (pre-filled ZIP + radius, no login) has **no clean precedent** among peer products, but the pattern that comes closest — Yik Yak's 1.5-mile GPS geofence around K-12 campuses — shows the mechanism works technically for *drawing* a boundary but says nothing about *identity*, and Yik Yak's problems (bullying, threats) stemmed precisely from having geofencing without accountability. Every product that has actually managed "local" trust at Keepsake's transaction stakes (in-person handoff of physical goods) pairs geography with an identity or reputation signal: Nextdoor requires address verification (postcard/phone/card), Karrot requires recurring GPS re-verification plus a public reputation score ("manner temperature"), Freecycle/Buy Nothing lean on volunteer human moderators who challenge out-of-area posters, and even Craigslist — the closest "no-login" analog — layered on phone verification and IP/geo-fraud detection only *after* rampant spam forced the issue in 2008 [verified by fetch pattern via search: Techdirt/TechCrunch]. For a two-campus Houston pilot, the minimum credible pattern is: (1) a verified email per giver/requester as Keepsake already requires, (2) a lightweight geo-plausibility check (IP-geolocation cross-check against the claimed ZIP, the way Craigslist flags an SF post from a Miami IP), and (3) human/community reporting with a visible reputation or feedback history (Trash Nothing's model), rather than relying on ZIP + radius alone to constitute "campus." Strongest source: Techdirt/Trust & Safety Foundation's writeup of Craigslist's 2008 phone-verification rollout, and Yik Yak's documented Maponics-based geofence.

## Findings

- **Craigslist phone verification (2008)**: Introduced first for "erotic services" to fight spam/prostitution ads, then expanded; callers get a one-time code, VoIP numbers barred, 3 verification calls/day/account cap. [verified by fetch pattern] — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/2021/04/30/content-moderation-case-study-craigslist-implements-phone-verification-to-fight-spam-spammers-fight-back-2008/), [TechCrunch, 2008-07-17](https://techcrunch.com/2008/07/17/craigslist-lockdown-site-with-phone-verification/)
- **Craigslist geo/IP fraud detection**: Craigslist runs "GEO IP detection" to catch out-of-area/out-of-country posters; posting in one city while IP resolves to another is treated as a red flag; known VPN/datacenter IP ranges get blocked. [search snippet only] — [SpyderProxy](https://spyderproxy.com/blog/how-to-unblock-craigslist), [Warrior Forum thread](https://www.warriorforum.com/off-topic-forum/132493-craigslist-geo-ip-detection-sufferer.html)
- **Nextdoor address verification**: Three methods — postcard (3–7 business days), phone match to a phone-bill address, or credit/debit card billing-address match; a "neighbor vouch" feature lets existing verified members manually vouch for a struggling neighbor. No specific 2023–2025 change documented in search results. [search snippet only] — [Nextdoor guide, 2024](https://nextdoorjoin.com/the-guide-to-using-nextdoor-2024.html)
- **Karrot/Danggeun Market**: Requires GPS re-verification of neighborhood ("Dong") roughly every 30 days to keep trading privileges; trading radius ~6 km; reputation via "manner temperature" starting at 36.5°C, rising with good activity, falling on policy violations/no-shows. [search snippet only] — [Seoulstart guide](https://seoulstart.com/guides/karrot-danggeun-guide), [Harvard D3 case study](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/a-closer-look-at-south-koreas-beloved-neighborhood-market-platform/)
- **Front Porch Forum (Vermont)**: Requires a street address at registration; each of ~250 town/district forums is professionally moderated (12 moderators among ~30 staff, ~250,000 members as of 2025). Small, staffed, non-anonymous — the opposite design point from a no-login model. [search snippet only] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_Porch_Forum)
- **Olio**: Relies on user profiles, SMS verification, "ambiguous" (fuzzed) GPS pickup coordinates, and star ratings — not strict address/GPS proof of residence. [search snippet only] — [Olio getting-started page](https://olioapp.com/en/getting-started-on-olio/what-is-olio/)
- **Trash Nothing / Freecycle**: Groups are run by volunteer local moderators who may screen new-member requests (asking cross-streets or ZIP, never full address) specifically "to prevent spammers and non-local people from joining"; reported members/posts can be banned; feedback scores are visible. [search snippet only] — [Freecycle Mod Manual](https://www.freecycle.org/pages/ModManual/LocalDecisions), [Trash Nothing Help](https://help.trashnothing.com/)
- **Buy Nothing Project**: Groups are hyperlocal by design (often oddly specific neighborhood boundaries in cities); enforcement is by volunteer neighborhood admins with varying strictness; no money/resale allowed. Search did not surface documented "picker/flipper" enforcement language specifically. [search snippet only] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_Nothing_Project), [DCist, 2023](https://dcist.com/story/23/02/21/buy-nothing-capitol-hill-take-my-sht-group-drama/)
- **Yik Yak geofencing**: Posting radius ~1.5 miles; partnered with data provider Maponics (GPS coordinates for ~85% of US schools) to disable the app on/near K-12 grounds after cyberbullying and bomb-threat-hoax incidents (e.g., San Clemente, CA). Shows geofencing is buildable, but Yik Yak's core failure was anonymity, not the fence. [search snippet only] — [NetFamilyNews](https://www.netfamilynews.org/yik-yak-update-app-came-geo-fence-us-schools), [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/29709592/app-uses-geo-fencing-to-block-bullies)
- **Tinder U**: Required .edu email verification plus on-campus geolocation to enroll — identity (.edu) always paired with geography, never geography alone. [search snippet only] — [Tinder Help Center](https://www.help.tinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015516052-Tinder-U-College-Mode)
- **Rice–UH distance**: ~6 miles apart; fastest documented time ~9 minutes by car/taxi, ~48 minutes by public bus (line 004). [search snippet only, could not fetch primary maps source — Google Maps fetch returned no data] — [Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Rice-University/University-of-Houston)
