# Dating-App Fatigue → In-Person, Activity-Based Dating: Research for Keepsake

**Verdict:** This is a strong, well-evidenced angle. Two independent trend lines converge cleanly on Keepsake's format: (1) hard usage/financial declines at Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge alongside survey data on Gen Z "swipe burnout," and (2) a documented, fast-growing shift toward *activity-based* IRL meetups (run clubs, Timeleft dinners, Pitch-a-Friend, Eventbrite "meet-cute" events) where the *activity itself*, not an explicit dating intent, is the pretext for meeting strangers. The single best piece of evidence is the CBS News / Boston Globe run-club reporting (confirmed by direct fetch): singles are explicitly choosing low-stakes, activity-first, judgment-free formats over swiping, with named clubs (Lunge Run Club, Brickell Run Club) growing from dozens to hundreds of weekly attendees specifically because it removes app-style evaluation pressure. Keepsake's browse-by-postcode / "come see what's free" model is structurally identical to a run club or thrift date: strangers co-present around a shared, low-stakes activity, with romance an optional byproduct rather than the stated goal — which is precisely the "third place" gap discourse says people want filled.

## Risks / objections
- Much of this is trend journalism (PureWow, lifestyle press) recycling the same few stats (78% burnout, Ofcom numbers) without primary-source links — several key pages (Forbes Health, Ofcom, CNBC, datingnews) returned 403 and could not be independently verified by fetch.
- No evidence yet directly links thrifting/estate sales to *dating intent* at scale — that connection is journalistic anecdote (TikTok trend, "date idea" listicles) rather than platform data; ThredUp/EstateSales.net data measure resale economics, not romance.
- Houston-specific evidence for this exact angle is thin — no local press has covered "thrifting as a Houston date trend" or antique-mall singles events specifically.
- Risk of over-claiming Keepsake as a "dating app": item-giving is asymmetric (giver picks; interest is one-directional via notes) and not framed for romance — leaning too hard into the date angle could misrepresent the product or invite unwanted flirtation risk in a handoff meant to be safe and low-stakes.

## Three ways Keepsake could use this angle
1. Houston launch PR/content pegged to "run-club energy for objects" — position browsing the feed or attending a handoff as a no-pressure, activity-first way to meet neighbors, citing the run-club and Eventbrite "meet-cute" data as proof people want a pretext, not a swipe.
2. Partner with or get featured alongside Houston antique/thrift venues (Antique Gallery of Houston, Charity Guild Shop) as a "browse together" outing — pitch local lifestyle press a "new Houston date idea: swap stories over a keepsake" story, riding the existing thrift-date-idea listicle format.
3. Add a lightweight "browse together" mode or shareable feed link framed for pairs/friends doing a "keepsake crawl," explicitly marketed as an alternative third place — not a dating feature, but adjacent enough to ride the slow-dating/third-place discourse in press outreach without repositioning the core product.
