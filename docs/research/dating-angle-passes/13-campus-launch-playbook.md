# Campus Launch Playbooks for Keepsake — Research Findings

## Verdict

The cheapest credible first-campus playbook for Keepsake combines two proven, low/no-budget tactics rather than one: (1) a **flyer/word-of-mouth seed with a founder physically walking the campus** (Yik Yak at Furman, Snackpass at Yale — both started with founders postering and emailing student orgs, zero ad spend), plus (2) a **student-newspaper "new thing on campus" story** as the amplification channel, which is documented and repeatable (Rice Thresher covered Nudge, a two-sophomore social app, on its Feb. 24, 2025 launch — no PR firm, no budget mentioned). The best single piece of evidence is the Yik Yak founders' documented approach: two students "promoted the early release by emailing student organizations, fraternities, and sororities, leveraging word-of-mouth among peers to seed adoption without broader marketing campaigns," which produced growth from ~30 to ~300 schools within weeks (TechCrunch/Digiday). This transfers cleanly to Keepsake because it required no native app, no paid staff, and no .edu login — it depended only on identifiable local student groups willing to spread a link, which a web-only, no-account gifting site can replicate exactly via a "Rice edition" landing page and manual outreach to a handful of clubs and the Thresher.

## Findings

- **Facebook (Harvard → Ivies, 2004):** Launched Feb 4, 2004 at Harvard, expanded to Columbia, Stanford, Yale within weeks (March 2004), reaching most US/Canada campuses by year end. Growth depended entirely on requiring a verified school email per campus — a mechanism Keepsake explicitly lacks. [search snippet only] — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Facebook
- **Tinder (USC/SMU, 2012):** Whitney Wolfe "crashed sorority meetings," had members install the app, then showed the matching fraternity the app full of women they knew; grew from 5,000 to 15,000 users during her college tour before moving to SMU. Depended on native app + real photos/identity, and the sorority/fraternity social structure as the "proof" mechanic — not transferable to an anonymous item-gifting site. [search snippet only] — techcrunch.com/2014/07/09/whitney-wolfe-vs-tinder
- **Fizz (Stanford, 2021):** Founders Teddy Solomon and Ashton Cofer built it post-pandemic for connection; ambassadors tabled and gave "a free donut in exchange for downloads" — explicitly called "pretty common for startups targeting college students." Verification is by .edu email, and banned users can't return because they have no second school email. Reached "95% of Stanford's ~7,600 undergrads." Cost model elsewhere: some paid moderators $500/month, some ambassadors paid $50 + $25 bonus per 600 signups. Depends on .edu gating and a native app; Keepsake has neither, but the donut-for-download tabling tactic itself transfers. [verified by fetch] — techcrunch.com/2022/10/04/fizz-app-college-stanford-social/
- **Yik Yak (2013–14):** Founded by two Furman University students; launched Nov 6, 2013; spread from Furman to Georgia Tech to UGA via "emailing student organizations, fraternities, and sororities." Went from >30 schools to >300 in the weeks after spring break 2014 via guerrilla, on-the-ground flyering by founders/early team, no ad spend documented. No .edu requirement, no accounts (anonymous). This is the closest structural analog to Keepsake. [search snippet only] — digiday.com/media/inside-yik-yak-anonymous-sharing-app-sweeping-colleges/, techcrunch.com/2014/02/19
- **Marriage Pact (Stanford, 2017):** Two econ students, Liam McGregor and Sophia Sterling-Angus, emailed ~10 Stanford lists/group chats as a class project; got 1,000+ responses in 13 hours, 4,100+ (nearly 60% of undergrads) within days, "turning away" students from other schools. Expanded from fall 2020 onward via a decentralized model of local student reps per campus who rework survey questions for their school (confirmed for Rice, Notre Dame) — reached 65 campuses in 13 months, 109 by 2025. No app, no accounts, just an emailed form — highly transferable mechanic (viral emailed link) but relies on class mailing-list access Keepsake doesn't have. [verified by fetch, partial search] — stanfordmag.org/contents/if-romance-goes-sideways-this-algorithm-might-help
- **Datamatch (Harvard→Brown/Columbia/Wellesley, 2018):** Expansion tactic was partnering with each campus's own humor/satire publication (e.g., Columbia's The Jester) to write localized survey questions and spread awareness, rather than tech clubs. Wellesley got a discounted-date sponsorship from a local yogurt shop; no universal cost or verification details found. Model: partner with an existing student publication as local co-owner — directly transferable to Keepsake as a "campus editor" model. [verified by fetch] — thecrimson.com/article/2018/2/7/datamatch-expands/
- **Snackpass (Yale, 2016):** First 100 customers came from flyers founders personally put up around campus; grew by going door-to-door to local restaurants. No ambassador budget mentioned at this stage — pure founder legwork. [search snippet only] — dormroomfund.medium.com/how-kevin-tan-and-jamie-marshall-built-snackpass
- **Bumble campus ambassadors:** Structured "Honey"/"Campus Director" program, paid monthly + bonuses per download, requires ongoing paid staffing — not zero-budget. [search snippet only] — laloyolan.com, columbiaspectator.com
- **Handshake:** Grew via founders literally driving campus to campus meeting career-center directors in person ("Ford Focus" strategy) with small personalized gifts — a B2B/institutional sale, not a peer-to-peer viral seed; not directly transferable to Keepsake's grassroots model but validates in-person, founder-led outreach as cheap and effective. [search snippet only] — ainativegtm.substack.com
- **Rumie (Ole Miss, 2022):** College marketplace requiring sign-in with college-affiliated email so users are "bound to their school's code of conduct" — the .edu-gated trust mechanism Keepsake explicitly forgoes. [search snippet only] — campussafetymagazine.com
- **Buy Nothing Project:** No campus-specific chapter model found; general neighborhood Facebook-group gift economy, no evidence of a college-specific playbook. **Not found.**
- **Student newspaper coverage — confirmed and recent:** The Rice Thresher ran "'Real life sort of friendship': Nudge app launches at Rice" (published ~March 2025) about a social app built by two Rice sophomores that launched Feb. 24, funded via the campus Lilie Lab's Launchpad program — direct evidence student papers do run "new app on campus" pieces with no PR spend required. [search snippet only, direct fetch blocked 403] — ricethresher.org/article/2025/03/real-life-sort-of-friendship-nudge-app-launches-at-rice
- **Daily Cougar (UH):** Could not find a specific "new app launched at UH" story from The Cougar/Daily Cougar in this search pass. **Not found** — recommend a follow-up search closer to pitch time.
- **The Varsity (U of T):** Coverage found of student startups generally (CheaprEats, Weav, Hypercare) via the university's Hub competition and Entrepreneurship Week, not spontaneous "new site" coverage — suggests campus accelerator visibility, not cold-pitch coverage, is the actual channel there. [search snippet only]
- **Tabling/solicitation rules:** Common pattern across Villanova, Brown, Tufts, U Minnesota Duluth: an outside/non-university partner needs a Registered Student Organization (or department) sponsor, a student rep from that org present the entire time, and the partner's purpose must connect to the sponsoring org's mission; commercial solicitation by outside vendors generally requires prior written approval. [search snippet only, multiple university policy pages] — tufts.edu, villanova.edu, kirby.d.umn.edu
- **.edu/geofence scoping without login:** No product found that scopes strictly by ZIP-code radius around a campus with zero account (Keepsake's own model). Comparable products (Rumie, Fizz, Sidechat) all use .edu email as the trust/scoping mechanism instead of geography — meaning Keepsake's postcode-radius, no-account model is largely untested territory rather than a proven pattern. **Not found** — no direct precedent located.
- **Failures/backlash:** Yik Yak — 70+ women's/civil-rights groups petitioned the Dept. of Education in 2015 over threats/harassment (Univ. of Mary Washington rape/murder threats, Clemson racist posts); at least half a dozen campuses banned it outright (Vermont, New Mexico, Chicago-area, Drake, Iowa State, U Iowa, UNI); app shut down 2017, IP sold to Square for $1M. [search snippet only] — foxnews.com, endthebull.wordpress.com. Fizz — Nov 2021, three Stanford students found a security flaw letting anyone query the database directly and deanonymize posts; fixed, but reputational damage documented in Stanford Daily. [verified by fetch] — stanforddaily.com/2022/11/01. Sidechat — accidentally let non-students access the platform (May 2023); Harvard demanded better moderation after antisemitic posts (Jan 2024); UNC Chapel Hill blocked it on campus wifi. [search snippet only] — insidehighered.com, yahoo.com

## Risks/Objections

- No precedent was found for Keepsake's exact model (postcode-radius scoping, zero accounts, verified email only at the point someone claims an item) — every comparable campus product substitutes .edu login or a native app for the trust/scoping layer Keepsake is trying to do without, so the "campus" framing may need a manual, human-curated proxy (e.g., a founder-run "Rice items only" filter) rather than a technical geofence.
- Anonymous, unmoderated campus platforms (Yik Yak, Sidechat) show that low-moderation-cost models can escalate into harassment and formal bans; Keepsake's model requires an email + a story + a name per giver, which likely mitigates this, but a solo operator has no moderation staff if something goes wrong.
- Student-newspaper coverage (Rice Thresher/Nudge) is a documented channel but is a single data point from a search snippet, not independently fetched (403 on direct access) — confidence is moderate, not high.
- Tabling/solicitation rules generally require a registered student org sponsor for an outside operator; a solo, unaffiliated founder cannot table without finding a club willing to co-host, which is a real cost (time, relationship-building) not captured in the "$0" line above.
