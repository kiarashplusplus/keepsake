# Insurance Research: P2P Gifting Marketplace (Keepsake)

## Verdict

The realistic shape for a one-person Texas sole-proprietor gifting site is a small-business BOP/GL package (~$300–800/yr territory is plausible per multiple 2026 cost surveys) **plus** a standalone tech E&O/cyber add-on if the broker has one, because standard ISO CGL forms don't clearly answer the one risk that matters here — a stranger getting hurt during a handoff arranged through the platform. No source found directly states that "facilitating in-person meetings between strangers" is a named CGL exclusion; instead the exposure falls into gaps between exclusions (professional services, abuse/molestation, assault & battery) that carriers handle inconsistently for tiny non-transactional platforms. **The one question to put to the broker verbatim:** *"If two strangers meet in person because of a listing on my site and one assaults or robs the other, does my policy respond at all, or does it fall into the assault-and-battery/abuse-and-molestation exclusion the same way it would for a bar — and if so, is there an endorsement or a specialty product (like CFC's Safeprotect) that actually covers user-on-user harm during an in-person meetup, not just misconduct by my own staff?"** Strongest source for the gap: CFC's Safeprotect Misconduct Liability product description confirms abuse/molestation products are built for *organization-against-third-party* wrongful acts (staff, volunteers), not clearly for *user-against-user* harm during a platform-arranged meetup — [cfc.com/safeprotect-misconduct-liability](https://www.cfc.com/en-us/products/class/safeprotect-misconduct-liability/) [verified by fetch, undated].

## Findings

- ISO CGL forms carry an **assault and battery exclusion** as standard/routine on most GL and liquor policies, requiring a separate endorsement or standalone A&B policy; a standalone A&B policy runs **$3,000–$8,000/yr for a mid-sized bar** (not a marketplace-specific figure). — americanbar.org, ABA Tort Trial journal, 2023 [search snippet only]
- **Abuse and molestation exclusion (ISO CG 21 46)** explicitly excludes bodily injury/property/personal injury from actual or threatened abuse or molestation; carriers increasingly attach it to commercial and personal liability policies, and it can be added back via endorsement (limits typically $100K–$1M) if the business shows a formal abuse-prevention plan — no cost figure for a small platform found. — amwins.com [verified by fetch, undated]; childcarecomp.com cites $750–$2,500/yr BOP baseline for small childcare orgs before such endorsements [search snippet only, different vertical]
- **Electronic data exclusion (ISO CGL exclusion "p")** bars coverage for damage/loss of electronic data itself, with a carve-back if that data loss causes bodily injury — this is why cyber is sold separately, not why in-person harm is excluded. — claimsjournal.com/carltonfields.com summaries [search snippet only]
- **Professional services / designer exclusion (CG 22 99 series)** excludes bodily injury/property damage "arising out of any act, error or omission" in website design/consulting — tech E&O is marketed as the "gap filler" for exactly this hole, not for user-to-user physical harm. — rmmagazine.com, irmi.com [search snippet only]
- Marketplace-specific industry guidance frames the standard stack as **GL (bodily injury/property damage tied to transactions) + Tech E&O (platform performance) + Cyber (user data) + D&O**, with **"trust and safety" coverage** explicitly named as covering "negligent screening... failure to moderate... liability when a transaction leads to injury" for in-person marketplaces like Turo/Airbnb; cost estimate cited **$2,000–$15,000/yr** for marketplace insurance generally (not solo-scale). — dittofi.com/corgi.insure, undated [search snippet only, could not verify against a primary carrier doc]
- **Vouch** (startup-focused carrier/MGA) explicitly underwrites peer-to-peer marketplaces (cited example: Neighbor, a P2P self-storage marketplace) and offers "vicarious liability coverage... covers disputes between platform participants" — the closest concrete evidence of a named carrier actively covering P2P platform disputes. — vouch.us/verticals/consumer [search snippet only, could not verify exact policy language by fetch]
- **CFC Underwriting** has an established appetite for dating apps specifically, citing insured "well-known dating apps," via a social-media/tech-media package with bodily-injury coverage "up to the full policy limit," but a specialist tech/media broker is recommended because "unique risks" require it — no marketplace-vs-dating distinction spelled out. — strategic-risk-global.com / insurancetimes.co.uk, undated [search snippet only]
- Hiscox, Next, Thimble, Embroker, Founder Shield, Coalition, biBERK, Hartford: **no published appetite guide, application question, or underwriting text using the terms "peer-to-peer," "marketplace," "users meet in person," or "dating" was found for any of them** despite targeted searches. This is a real gap, not just an oversight — [could not verify / not found].
- Craigslist's ToS: "any risk of using CL is assumed by you"; disclaims "liability or responsibility for acts, omissions, or conduct of you or any party in connection with CL" — no specific in-person safety clause in the ToS itself (safety guidance lives on a separate help page). — craigslist.org/about/terms.of.use [verified by fetch, undated]
- Freecycle (501(c)(3), Arizona): members "hold neither the list owners, moderators nor anyone affiliated with Freecycle.org responsible... use the list at their own risk," and explicitly flags the "potential risk of having 'a stranger' come to your home." — freecycle.org/faq.freecycle.org [search snippet only]
- Buy Nothing Project, Olio, Bunz: all carry nearly identical "no liability for user conduct / trade at your own risk / cannot assist with off-platform disputes" language — a consistent industry norm across every gift/trade P2P platform checked. — buynothingproject.org, olioapp.com, bunz.com [search snippet only]
- Freegle (UK charity) insurance posture: could not find Freegle-specific coverage; general UK guidance confirms charities buy **trustee indemnity insurance** to protect trustees personally from breach-of-duty claims (excludes criminal/dishonest/reckless acts) — this protects trustees, not users who meet in person. — getindemnity.co.uk et al. [search snippet only, Freegle-specific policy not found]
- **Dart v. Craigslist** and related cases: Section 230 has repeatedly shielded Craigslist from liability for third-party content (erotic services ads, discriminatory housing ads). — eff.org, Wikipedia [search snippet only]
- **Doe v. Internet Brands / Model Mayhem (9th Cir.)**: Section 230 did **not** bar a failure-to-warn claim where the platform allegedly knew specific predators were using it to lure victims to fake in-person meetings — a real precedent that a P2P/meetup platform can face liability for what it *knew and didn't warn about*, independent of user-generated content immunity. — harvardlawreview.org, blog.ericgoldman.org [search snippet only]
- No lawsuit was found holding OfferUp or Facebook Marketplace liable for a robbery/killing during a meetup — extensive criminal prosecutions of robbers exist, but **no civil platform-liability case found**. [not found]
- Airbnb: guest liability insurance ($1M) explicitly **excludes criminal acts and intentional acts like assault** — directly analogous to what a GL/A&B exclusion would do to Keepsake. — askadamskutner.com [search snippet only]
- Homeowners "business pursuits" exclusion: bars coverage for continuous, for-profit activity; **an "incidental business" endorsement can restore it, but one source states a sole proprietor (or partner) generally cannot add business-pursuits coverage to a homeowners policy this way** — meaning the operator's home policy likely will not cover this venture at all, endorsement or not. — theclm.org [verified by fetch, undated] — this specific "sole proprietors can't get the endorsement" claim came from a search summary, not a direct quote in the fetched article, so flag as **[could not verify precisely]**.
- Texas DBA/assumed name: no state-mandated insurance interaction found; practical guidance says only to "make sure your liability insurance company includes your assumed name" on the policy declarations. — legalatoms.com et al. [search snippet only]
- Cost benchmarks: multiple 2026 small-business surveys converge on **$300–$1,500/yr** for solo/low-risk GL, with **$300–$500/yr** typical for solo, website-based, non-professional-services businesses — consistent with the $300–800 sanity-check band already cited to the operator. — moneygeek.com, quotesweep.com [search snippet only]

## Application questions to expect (and the truthful answer)

- *"Do users transact for money on your platform?"* → No — free gifting only, no fees, no payment processing.
- *"Do users meet in person as a result of your service?"* → Yes — this is the core mechanic (handoff between stranger giver/requester); expect this to trigger a marketplace/trust-and-safety or assault-and-battery underwriting question, not a standard box on a generic BOP app.
- *"Do you screen, verify, or background-check users?"* → No — anyone can browse by postcode with no account; contact is only revealed by email after selection.
- *"Is there a dating, social, or romantic-matching component?"* → No — but note the mechanic (two strangers matched to meet in person) resembles what dating-app underwriters (CFC) specifically price for, so expect the broker to ask anyway.
- *"Do you operate as a sole proprietor from a home address?"* → Yes — expect follow-up on whether a homeowners "business pursuits" exclusion or an "incidental business" endorsement is already in place, and whether the assumed-name/DBA is listed on the policy.
