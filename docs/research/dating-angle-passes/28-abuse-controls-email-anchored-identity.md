# Email-Anchored Identity: Abuse Controls Research

**Verdict:** The strongest single data point is Cloudflare's own geolocation documentation (fetched directly): *"IP geolocation is an estimate, not an exact science... do not use IP geolocation as the sole signal"* for anything precision-critical. That one sentence sets the ceiling for everything else in this brief — email-anchored, login-free platforms (Craigslist, Trash Nothing, Freecycle, Olio) never had precise location or persistent identity either, and they converged on the same minimum viable stack: (a) a *verified* contact channel per party (email or phone) as the only durable handle, (b) rate/frequency limits on that channel rather than on "accounts," (c) community reporting + human moderation for content, and (d) soft-flagging of location mismatches rather than hard blocks, because no one — including Cloudflare and MaxMind — claims IP/postal signals are reliable enough to gate on alone. For Keepsake's two-campus pilot, the available, non-invented signals are: giver email hash + ban (already built), requester email + per-address daily confirmation cap (already built), requester's email domain (.edu observable only as a *soft* trust/pre-fill signal, not a gate, matching the "no .edu gating" decision), and IP-region-vs-claimed-postcode as a soft flag. There is no comparable precedent for banning an anonymous demand-side actor by anything other than their one verified contact point — every platform reviewed either removes content, throttles the channel, or (Craigslist, Front Porch Forum) requires a name/relay identity that Keepsake has deliberately declined to build.

## Findings 2–6

**2. Email as an anti-abuse signal.** The most commonly cited open-source disposable-domain list (ivolo/disposable-email-domains, feeding the `disposable-email-domains` npm package) is now **unmaintained** — the maintainer's README points users to a fork — meaning any static list needs an active source (e.g., Kickbox's live lookup API) rather than a vendored snapshot. Commercial verifiers (ZeroBounce, Kickbox, NeverBounce, Emailable) all bundle disposable-domain + MX-record checks but do not open-source their lists. On institutional-domain risk: one industry source (Riskified) states .edu-domain orders are "low risk," about 0.62% of e-commerce transactions, while also flagging that "there are entire websites devoted to how to create a fake .edu address" — so .edu is a *reducer* of risk, not a guarantee. No study was found quantifying free-mail vs .edu abuse rates specifically for peer-to-peer gifting/marketplace platforms — **not found**. On rate limits: Cloudflare's WAF rate-limiting rules operate per-request-attribute (IP, header, cookie) at the edge, and its own docs note that pure client-side gates (Turnstile) are bypassed by direct POSTs, so **both** an app-level per-address cap (Keepsake's "3 confirmations/address/day") and an edge per-IP rate limit are recommended together, not as substitutes — no published comparison of the two approaches' relative cost was found.

**3. No-requester-ban trade-off.** No platform reviewed bans anonymous demand-side actors by any handle other than their one verified contact channel. Freecycle's own moderator manual explicitly *instructs moderators not to keep public no-show/problem-member lists* — the same restraint Keepsake has chosen. Buy Nothing solves it structurally by giving the giver unilateral "gifter's choice" veto instead of vetting requesters. Craigslist's relay anonymizes both sides symmetrically rather than tracking either. Hash-of-email blocklisting (the pattern HIBP's k-anonymity model demonstrates for passwords, and Spamhaus's hash blocklists demonstrate for compromised mailboxes) is a legitimate, privacy-preserving precedent for banning an *address* without minting a persistent identifier — directly analogous to what Keepsake already does for the requester email-confirmation gate. No data was found quantifying repeat-abuse rate from a single email address on a gifting platform specifically — **not found**.

**4. Location plausibility.** Cloudflare's own docs (verified by fetch) state city/postal-level geolocation is estimate-only, degraded by VPNs, and explicitly *not* to be used as a sole signal for anything precision-critical; MaxMind's public accuracy-comparison tool reports its web-service lookups beat its database product by "5% more IPs at postal-code level, 2% more at city level" in the US, but publishes no absolute accuracy percentage in the search results retrieved — exact figures **not found**, tool exists at maxmind.com/en/geoip-accuracy-comparison for direct query. Cloudflare's Managed Transforms can inject `cf-ipcountry`, `cf-region`, `cf-ipcity`, `cf-postal-code` headers, sourced from IPinfo. Karrot is the one precedent (verified by fetch) for *actual* GPS-verified location rather than claimed/IP-inferred — but it explicitly fails for secondary/VOIP numbers, a real risk for a student population. The consistent industry pattern is: use IP/postal mismatch as a **soft flag for human review**, never a hard block — consistent with Keepsake's proposed design.

**5. Reputation without profiles.** Trash Nothing's feedback score (net positive/negative + recent-year percentage) is the clearest precedent that a completed-transaction count can be shown without a browsable profile page. Craigslist has no "posted by" identity at all. Buy Nothing's *app* version deliberately removed the Facebook-era ability to click into a person's profile — evidence that dropping profile-like affordances is a live, accepted design choice among peers, not an outlier. Whether a bare completed-gift counter still trips the SCOPE Act's "profile" prong is a legal question outside this research's scope — flagged only, not analyzed.

**6. Campus pilot scoping.** The only genuinely new signal available at a campus pilot's confirmation step is the requester's .edu domain — matching Fizz/Rumie/Marriage Pact's approach, except those three treat .edu as a **hard gate** (no non-.edu account is created at all), whereas Keepsake's own design explicitly avoids .edu gating and would use it only as a soft signal alongside postcode pre-fill and IP-region. What Fizz/Rumie's hard .edu gate actually prevents that Keepsake's soft version would not: any *non-student* browsing or requesting in the pilot's postcode radius at all — Keepsake's radius-based pilot (Rice/UH postcodes, no .edu gate) by design permits non-student neighbors to participate, which is a scope decision, not a security gap.
