# Keepsake Competitive Scan: Objects, Stories & Connection

## Verdict

The graveyard is real but instructive in a specific way: nearly every "swipe on an object/hobby" dating app (Ship, Sweatt, Twine, Loveflutter, Tastebuds) either died or stalled into irrelevance, because bolting a gimmick onto Tinder's swipe-and-match mechanic doesn't fix dating apps' core trust and effort problems — it just adds a filter. The survivors and the genuinely large platforms all cluster around a different pattern: async, low-stakes, no-swipe formats (Lex's text-first posts, PostSecret's postcards, Olio's gifting-plus-forum, Timeleft's structured dinners) that let a story or object be the *reason people show up*, not the matching algorithm itself. The single most instructive case is **Olio** (9M+ downloads, community forum, event listings, self-reported mental-health lift) — it proves a free-gifting marketplace *can* generate durable community and even friendship without ever claiming to be a dating product, and without a "who gets the item" mechanic becoming a beauty contest. Keepsake's safest, most differentiated path is to stay explicitly non-romantic (as it's designed) and treat any relationship-adjacent outcomes as an unadvertised byproduct — leaning into "dating" risks importing the exact swipe/rejection dynamics that killed the object-based dating category, and risks turning "who gets the item" into something uncomfortable given real names/emails are exchanged.

## Findings

- Ship (Betches x Match Group), launched 2019: friend-swipes-for-you dating app; the current company site states it is "no longer available." Exact shutdown date not found. [search snippet only] — https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/news/match-groups-ship-no-longer-available/
- Loveflutter, ~2013–2014: personality/blurred-photo dating app, later rebooted as a "quirkier Tinder" (TechCrunch); current live/dead status undetermined from available sources. [search snippet only] — https://techcrunch.com/?p=1004115
- Twine, 2013–2014: "Tinder based on personality not sex appeal" (TechCrunch); some listings persist but no evidence of meaningful current activity. [search snippet only] — https://techcrunch.com/2014/01/31/twine-canvas-is-like-tinder-but-based-on-personality-not-sex-appeal
- Tastebuds.fm, music-taste matching: announced shutdown for April 18, 2023, reprieved briefly by user demand, but reported as no longer operating as of August 2025. [search snippet only]
- Sweatt, fitness dating, launched 2015: reviewer notes "dwindling user base" and "development has stalled." [search snippet only]
- Kippo, gaming dating app, launched Sept 2019: marketing describes "the OG Kippo is back," implying a hiatus/relaunch; app currently listed as active in 2026. [search snippet only]
- Lex, text-first queer social/dating app, launched 2020 ($1.5M raise): as of 2025, "millions of messages sent each month," users "in over 200 countries," named a top social app by Fast Company (2024) — the clearest survivor among niche-format apps, notably because it drops photos/objects-as-swipe-fodder for personal-ad-style text. [search snippet only]
- PostSecret, founded 2004: over 1 million secrets received, 700M+ site visits, $1M+ raised for suicide prevention — massive scale for anonymous story-sharing, but no evidence found of it generating meetups or relationships; it stayed deliberately anonymous/one-way. [search snippet only]
- The Strangers Project, founded 2009: 65,000–100,000+ handwritten stranger stories, traveling exhibit across 25+ cities — evidence of scale and emotional impact; no evidence found linking it to relationship formation. [search snippet only]
- Dear Stranger (Oregon Humanities), founded 2014: pen-pal-style letter exchange; "more than 1,100 Oregonians" have participated since inception, later expanded to Nebraska (program ended 2022) and Florida. Founder noted receiving letters had the biggest impact. Modest scale — illustrates that deep story-based connection formats are hard to scale past four figures without an app layer. [search snippet only]
- Buy Nothing app: after splitting from Facebook, the standalone app is described by users as "clunky, unreliable, and not community-minded," and has "yet to gain the same momentum" as the Facebook-hosted groups, where in-person meetups/swap sessions happen organically. Lesson: community/social features underperform when moved off an established social graph. [search snippet only]
- Olio: 9M+ downloads; users report forming friendships via its forum beyond item exchange, plus a local-events/meetup listing feature; 84% report improved financial wellbeing, 76% improved mental health (Olio's own reporting). [search snippet only, self-reported by company]
- Karrot (Danggeun Market): "Local Life" neighborhood board plus a 2026-launched "Cafe" interest-community feature (17 categories, community-manager monetization) show a resale/gifting marketplace evolving into a full local social layer with real traction (1M+ Canadian users cited separately). [search snippet only]
- Bumble BFF/Geneva: revamped 2025 into a groups-and-community app building on Geneva (acquired 2024, ~$17M); Bumble told investors Geneva "had not generated any revenue as of June 30, 2025" — a caution that community-friendship features don't monetize easily even inside a large dating company. [search snippet only]
- Timeleft: 150,000 monthly diners across 200+ cities, €18M ARR after 20 months (Aug 2025), ~100 employees, generating roughly $1M/week for local restaurants — the strongest proof that structured, low-effort IRL stranger-meeting scales commercially. [search snippet only]
- "Together tech" investment wave (TechCrunch, June 5, 2026, [verified by fetch]): Board (founded by Mirror's Brynn Putnam) raised $20M for in-person games/social experiences; article frames a broader founder trend of "building in the other direction" from AI, but names no object- or story-based connection startup specifically.
- Search for a live 2025–2026 startup explicitly pitching "meet people through the things you own or give away" (Product Hunt, TechCrunch, YC directory): not found.
- Bunz (object-barter marketplace): rebuilt after a 2018–2019 cryptocurrency-token pivot caused community revolt and layoffs; as of its own FAQ [verified by fetch], it remains independently, non-VC-operated and explicitly contingent on active-user survival, with a prior public statement that it would shut down by Sept. 1, 2026 absent 1,000 daily active users in one city — a live cautionary tale about token/gamification pivots poisoning a trust-based trading community.
