# Pass 12 — College dating culture 2024–2026 and the campus-scoped matching rituals that go viral

*Web research pass, 2026-09-09. Sonnet subagent, WebSearch + WebFetch only; no repository access. Brief: "Evidence of what college students want from meeting people and what formats have worked." Report reproduced verbatim below.*

---

## Verdict

College students are demonstrably burned out on swipe-based dating (79% Gen Z burnout, Forbes Health 2024) yet simultaneously starved for structured, low-stakes, in-person ways to connect — evidenced by the fact that the two campus rituals that *did* go viral (Marriage Pact, Datamatch/Perfect Match) succeed precisely because they are algorithmic, asynchronous, low-effort-to-enter, and social-proof-driven ("my friends are doing it"), not because they promise romance. The single best evidence for Keepsake's angle is Fizz's marketplace feature — a campus-verified, anonymous app that already fuses "trade stuff" and "reveal a crush" in one product, and its marketplace alone generated 50,000 listings and 150,000 DMs in three months (Mar–May 2024). That is a direct, real-world proof point that campus-scoped object exchange and anonymous social reach-out are already living in the same behavioral space students want — Keepsake's "leave a note, giver picks" mechanic is a natural, lower-stakes descendant of exactly that pattern, minus the swipe fatigue.

## Findings

**1. Dating app fatigue / "dating recession"**
- Forbes Health survey (published 2024, methodology not independently confirmed via fetch — site blocked WebFetch, so relying on aggregator quote): "79% of Gen Z users experience burnout from dating apps" — [search snippet only], https://www.forbes.com/health/dating/dating-app-fatigue/
- Jean Twenge / Monitoring the Future data (book *Generations*, 2024; X post Dec 2024): "In 2000, 80% of U.S. high school seniors had dated in the past year. By 2023, that number had dropped to 45%" — [search snippet only], https://x.com/jean_twenge/status/1868709720145772951
- Survey Center on American Life ("Gen Z's Romance Gap," Feb 9, 2024) — [verified by fetch]: "56 percent of Gen Z adults—and 54 percent of Gen Z men—said they were involved in a romantic relationship at any point during their teenage years," vs. 78% of Boomers and 76% of Gen X. https://www.americansurveycenter.org/commentary/gen-zs-romance-gap-why-nearly-half-of-young-men-arent-dating/
- Institute for Family Studies/YouGov survey — [search snippet only]: "74% of single U.S. women and 64% of single men in their 20s and early 30s have either never gone on a date before or have only dated a few times in the last year"; top cited barriers are money and lack of confidence. https://ifstudies.org/in-the-news/the-reason-gen-z-isnt-dating
- Hinge D.A.T.E. Report (Nov 2025, ~30,000 Hinge daters surveyed) — [search snippet only]: 84% of Gen Z daters "want new ways to build deeper emotional connections" but a "question deficit" holds them back. https://hinge.co/newsroom/2025-GenZ-Report
- The "57% of Gen Z not looking / attributed to Pew 2023" figure that circulates widely could not be pinned to an actual Pew report on fetch-level verification — multiple secondary sites cite it as Pew, but no primary Pew document surfaced. [could not verify] — treat this specific number as unconfirmed provenance.

**2. Campus matching rituals**
- Marriage Pact — [verified by fetch, Stanford Daily 2026 campus report + 2022 spread article]: founded 2017 by Stanford Econ 136 students Liam McGregor and Sophia Sterling-Angus; 2025 Stanford participation 4,177 students, 99% of seniors participate before graduating; spread to 60+ campuses within a year of a viral Duke TikTok (~100,000 likes); at Duke, ~4,500 students (two-thirds of undergrads) signed up within one week of launch; grown to 109 schools, 628,977 cumulative participants nationwide as of Nov 2025 [search snippet only for the 109/629k figure — https://grokipedia.com/page/Marriage_Pact]; at Penn 2024, 4,935 participants = 45%+ of undergrads (https://www.thedp.com/article/2024/10/penn-marriage-pact-fifth-year, snippet only). Business model: free to students, $5M seed funding (Bain Capital Ventures, Precursor Ventures, Centre Street Partners) per Stanford Daily 2022 (https://stanforddaily.com/2022/09/28/marriage-pact-secures-5-million-in-seed-funding/, snippet only); monetization still undecided, some universities reportedly sponsor it. Founder quote on virality: "I think most people sign up for the very fact that their friends are doing it." No Rice/UH participation found — [not found].
- Datamatch (Harvard-founded, 1994) — [verified by fetch, Harvard Crimson Feb 2025]: 2025 participation ~2,700 Harvard undergrads, down >1,400 from prior year after student-government funding was cut from $6,000 to $4,000; outcomes documented in the article were mostly platonic/friendly (matched with best friend, matched with roommate), not confirmed new romances. Expanded to 13 schools by 2019, "more than two dozen" by 2020 (Harvard Gazette, snippet only) — no evidence found of Rice or University of Houston participation [not found].
- Cornell Perfect Match — [search snippet only, Cornell Daily Sun multiple years]: started 2019 by student Jamal Hashim; over 5,000 students registered as of 2025; runs every Valentine's Day; anecdotal reports of students dating/still talking after being matched.
- No evidence found on candy-gram sales, student-run speed dating, or "Pitch a Friend" campus editions specifically — [not found] for those three sub-items.

**3. Anonymous campus crush/confession culture**
- Fizz — [verified by fetch, TechCrunch Jul 2024]: campus-verified, school-email-gated anonymous app; "Crush" is a built-in post category alongside "Question"; marketplace feature (added 2024) generated 50,000 listings and 150,000 direct messages between March–May 2024, sellers average two inquiries per listing; live on 80+ campuses (search snippet). This is the closest direct analog to Keepsake's campus + object + note mechanic already at scale.
- School-specific crush/confession Instagram accounts — [search snippet only]: @unccrushes (~500 posts as of Sept 2025), @nucrush (Northwestern, 40+ carousel posts since Feb 2019 launch), Wellesley.Crushes (since May 2020), LUC Crushes (Loyola). Aggregator @collegefessing has ~6M followers.
- Academic research on Facebook Confessions pages — [search snippet only]: one study analyzed ~200,000 confession posts over 3 years using topic modeling; another found ~26.1% of confessions involved student-to-student academic help-seeking. Sources: researchgate.net/publication/327945083 and arxiv.org/pdf/1506.05193.

**4. Senior wills & Greek big/little gift traditions**
- Senior wills — [search snippet only]: century-old tradition, typically at the high-school/prep-school level in the sources found (IMSA, Cistercian, various high school papers); students "bequeath" objects, traits, or advice to underclassmen, published in school newspapers/yearbooks annually. Could not confirm a widespread *college-newspaper* version (most concrete hits were secondary/high schools) — [could not verify] for the specifically collegiate framing in the brief.
- Greek big/little — [search snippet only, Something Greek, TCU 360, Greek Gear]: "Big/Little Week" is an established, emotionally charged ritual; bigs give littles physical gifts (paddles, crafts, personalized items) as "lasting mementos of the bond"; described as creating "emotional, unforgettable moments." Good qualitative evidence that campus culture already treats gifted objects as relationship-signifiers, but no data quantifying frequency or object-story transfer specifically.

**5. Meeting partners via free-stuff/marketplace/mutual-aid channels**
- No direct evidence found of college students meeting romantic partners specifically through free-stuff/Buy Nothing/marketplace channels — [not found]. One anecdotal, non-collegiate Buy Nothing story surfaced ("we know Amanda through the Buy Nothing group," https://www.aol.com/free-stuff-joining-buy-nothing-185034774.html) but it is not campus-specific and not clearly romantic.
- The closest and strongest proxy is Fizz's marketplace-plus-crush-feature co-existence on the same anonymous, campus-verified app (see #3), which is a structural analog even though no study directly measures dating outcomes from marketplace use.

## Risks / Objections
- Several widely-repeated Gen Z dating statistics (the "57% not looking," some Forbes Health figures) have murky or unconfirmed primary sourcing — secondary sites cite each other and attribution should be treated cautiously in any pitch deck.
- Campus rituals that spread fastest (Marriage Pact, Datamatch) are explicitly opt-in one-time surveys with near-zero effort and strong social proof/FOMO; Keepsake's flow (photograph, write a story, wait for notes, pick a winner) has more friction per use than either, so viral spread is not guaranteed to transfer.
- Fizz already occupies the "campus marketplace + anonymous crush" niche at 80+ campuses — any pitch should address why Keepsake's non-anonymous, story-driven, giver-picks model is differentiated rather than a slower-moving imitation.
- Datamatch's funding cut and declining participation (down >1,400 students YoY in 2025) show these rituals can lose momentum quickly without institutional/student-government backing — a caution against assuming automatic virality.
- No hard evidence exists yet that any of these mechanics actually produce dating relationships at scale (most outcomes documented are platonic/anecdotal), so pitching Keepsake explicitly as a "dating funnel" outpaces the evidence.

## 3 concrete ways Keepsake could use this
1. Borrow Marriage Pact's and Datamatch's core growth lever — a single, near-zero-friction seasonal moment (e.g., a "Move-Out Match" or finals-week keepsake drop) that students opt into en masse because friends are doing it, rather than relying on organic day-to-day listings.
2. Add a lightweight "Crush"-style tag or note option (as Fizz does) so a note left on a keepsake can double as a low-stakes reach-out, giving givers a reason to browse notes for more than just picking a recipient.
3. Partner with existing Greek life "big/little" and senior-will traditions by seeding Keepsake specifically at the end-of-year move-out and graduation windows, when students are already primed to hand meaningful objects to underclassmen with a story attached.
