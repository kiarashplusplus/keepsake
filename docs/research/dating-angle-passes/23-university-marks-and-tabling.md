# Keepsake: Rice / UH Campus-Presence Bottleneck — Research Findings

**Verdict.** At both schools, an unaffiliated, non-commercial site may *truthfully refer to* the university in body copy (e.g., "near Rice University," "for UH students") under nominative-fair-use case law, because that describes a fact the mark itself is needed to state — but neither school's own written policy says this; the policies instead govern *on-campus conduct* (posting, tabling, solicitation) and *brand/trademark use in marketing*, which are stricter and require permission. Concretely: Keepsake may say it serves "students near Rice" and "UH students" in its own marketing copy without a licence (strongest source: nominative-fair-use doctrine, Fifth Circuit's two-part test in *Pebble Beach Co. v. Tour 18 I Ltd.*, 155 F.3d 526 (5th Cir. 1998) — only so much of the mark as needed, no suggestion of affiliation). But it may **not** put "Rice" or "UH" into a campus-named URL, poster, or ad without written permission (Rice: brand.rice.edu/policies, verified by fetch — "prior permission is required for any internal or external individual, organization or company wishing to use Rice University's name... in any noncommercial manner," and Rice names/marks "may not be incorporated into off-campus... internet addresses or domain names"; UH: uh.edu/brand/brand-protection/external-users, verified by fetch — "third parties outside the UH System are only permitted to use UH marks under the terms of a... license agreement or by receiving express written permission"). Tabling, postering, and room booking on either campus require either registered-student-organization sponsorship or a written permission/reservation process with fees — an outsider cannot self-authorize any of it.

## Findings 3–5

- **Nominative fair use standard.** Ninth Circuit's *New Kids on the Block v. News America Publishing*, 971 F.2d 302 (1992): three-part test — (1) the referent isn't readily identifiable without the mark, (2) only so much of the mark as reasonably necessary is used, (3) nothing implies sponsorship/endorsement. Fifth Circuit (governs both Rice and UH, in Texas) uses a **two-part** variant from *Pebble Beach Co. v. Tour 18 I Ltd.*, 155 F.3d 526 (5th Cir. 1998): only so much of the mark as necessary, and no suggestion of affiliation/sponsorship/approval. Neither case involved a university; both support that plainly descriptive phrases like "near Rice University" or "for UH students," used without logos/colors and paired with a disclaimer, sit outside trademark liability — but this is doctrine applied by analogy, not a university-specific ruling. *Board of Supervisors for LSU v. Smack Apparel Co.*, 550 F.3d 465 (5th Cir. 2008) is a **different** fact pattern (merchandise using school colors/indicia held to have acquired secondary meaning, infringing) — it supports universities' merchandise/branding claims, not their ability to block truthful nominative references in unrelated non-merchandise contexts, and it cuts against Keepsake ever using Rice's or UH's colors/logos on its own materials.
- **Cease-and-desist precedents beyond TexAM.** Search did not surface a university-vs-unaffiliated-app cease-and-desist matching "College Confessions," Rate My Professors, or an unofficial "[School] Marketplace" app [could not verify / not found]. It did surface **Marriage Pact vs. Date Drop** (Nov. 2025) and **Penn Marriage Pact vs. Penn Date Drop** (cease-and-desist letters, Jan. 2026) — but these are **competitor-vs-competitor** disputes (alleged copying of Marriage Pact's own product/emails), not university-vs-app trademark actions [verified via Stanford Daily / Daily Pennsylvanian search snippets, not fetched].
- **How comparable apps handle it.** Fizz publishes an explicit disclaimer across its FAQ, Terms of Service, and Community Guidelines — "not affiliated with or connected to any educational institution" — and lets students set up a school's community "requiring no permission from the colleges" [search snippet only]. Sidechat operates similarly (student-email-gated, school-specific communities); no university trademark dispute was found for it, only Fizz-v-Sidechat unfair-competition litigation [search snippet only]. Marriage Pact and Datamatch brand primarily under their own names (marriagepact.com, "Datamatch") rather than "[School] Marriage Pact" as the lead mark; the precise per-school URL/subdomain convention was not independently confirmed [could not verify]. Storage Scholars takes the licensed/partnership route instead of nominative use — it's marketed as "the official moving partner of 90+ universities" and listed as an "approved vendor" on university housing sites [verified via search snippets from university pages, not independently fetched]; no equivalent disclaimer language was found for Dorm Room Movers [not found].
- **Student-org sponsorship route.** At both schools, a registered/recognized student organization can do materially more than an outsider: book rooms, table for free or at reduced/no cost, post flyers under the org's own name, and (at UH) even sponsor an outside group's table — but only by "actively participating" and taking on responsibility, not as a pass-through ("RSOs should not serve as fronts for off-campus groups... to obtain a reduced rental rate," uh.edu/studentcenters/reservations/rso, verified by fetch). At Rice, only "Eligible Persons" (students/faculty/staff/registered student organizations) can even file a demonstration or solicitation request; outsiders can appear only as named visitors in an approved request (policy.rice.edu/820, verified by fetch).
