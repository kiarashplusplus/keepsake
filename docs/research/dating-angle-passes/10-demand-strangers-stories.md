# Demand-Side Research: The "Romance of the Stranger" Angle for Keepsake

## Verdict

The demand side strongly supports the idea that people will browse strangers' sentimental-object stories for connection, not acquisition — but the strongest evidence is about *voyeuristic/narrative* pull (Humans of New York, PostSecret, Zillow surfing, estate-sale essays) rather than proof that this behavior converts into romantic/dating interest specifically. The single best piece of evidence is the **Facebook Marketplace data point that ~16% of users browse without ever buying**, combined with the **Sandstrom & Dunn weak-ties research showing casual/stranger interactions make up roughly 60% of people's daily social contact and independently boost belonging and wellbeing** — together these show (a) marketplaces already function partly as browsing/entertainment media, and (b) there is a real psychological mechanism (weak-tie contact) that low-stakes contact with strangers satisfies, which is exactly the emotional territory Keepsake's browse-and-note flow occupies. No source directly ties "stranger's object" browsing to *dating/romantic* outcomes — that inference is plausible but unverified.

## Risks / Objections

- Most of the strongest analogues (HONY, PostSecret, Missed Connections, Overheard) are about *reading strangers' words/photos*, not receiving their *physical objects* — the leap from "I like reading about strangers" to "I want to physically meet a stranger to collect their old lamp" is unproven.
- No evidence located ties any of this behavior to *dating or romantic* motivation specifically — Keepsake's "meet a stranger in person" mechanic could evoke curiosity/connection without evoking romance; conflating the two risks misreading the product's actual appeal.
- Loneliness and one-person-household stats are macro-level and not causally linked in any source to marketplace/object-story browsing behavior — this is an inference, not a documented behavior chain.
- Several figures (Marketplace's 16% browse-only, Cigna's 57%, thrift haul's 5.1B views) came from secondary/marketing aggregator sites, not primary sources, and should be treated as directional rather than citable statistics.

## Three Ways Keepsake Could Use This Angle

1. Build a public, no-account "browse the feed" mode (like Zillow surfing or PostSecret) explicitly marketed as entertainment/connection, not just a transaction tool — this taps the proven appetite for reading strangers' stories with zero friction.
2. Frame the "I'd love this" note-writing step as a version of Missed Connections/Overheard — a low-stakes, anonymous way to reach out to a stranger — since that framing is what readers say draws them to those formats.
3. In single-person-household-dense areas like Harris County (~31% one-person households), position Keepsake pickups as a sanctioned, low-stakes in-person encounter with a stranger, echoing Sandstrom & Dunn's finding that weak-tie contact measurably boosts belonging — without claiming or implying a dating outcome.
