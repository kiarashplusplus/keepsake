# Pass 05 — The adversary: what goes wrong when a marketplace gets a dating or flirting angle

*Web research pass, 2026-09-09. Sonnet subagent, WebSearch + WebFetch only; no repository access. Brief: "Be the adversary. Find evidence of what goes WRONG when a marketplace/gifting platform gets a dating or flirting angle, so Keepsake can decide whether a dating framing is safe." Not legal advice. Report reproduced verbatim below.*

---

## Verdict

A dating/flirting framing is **net-negative** for Keepsake and should be avoided. The strongest single piece of evidence is the pattern across Facebook Marketplace, Poshmark, and Vinted: platforms with zero dating intent still generate enough unwanted sexual/romantic contact between strangers meeting to exchange goods that they've had to build explicit anti-flirting policies and reporting tools after the fact (Vinted's "zero-tolerance" policy, Depop's "Sexually Inappropriate Content & Behaviour Policy," a February 2026 report of Vinted sellers organizing to demand harassment protections). Keepsake's model — free items, sentimental personal stories, in-person handoff, a giver choosing among people who "love" the object — reproduces the exact structural conditions (stranger contact, emotional narrative, physical meetup, no payment friction to deter bad actors) that already cause this problem on *non-romantic* platforms. Layering an explicit dating/flirting frame on top would not create a new risk so much as invite and legitimize the one these platforms already fight, while adding legal exposure (FOSTA-SESTA precedent, Grindr-style Section 230 litigation trends, insurance/liability treatment of "dating services") that a solo Texas sole proprietor has no ability to absorb.

Article notes Vinted has no automated filter for explicit messages, only after-the-fact reporting. [verified by fetch]

**Craigslist personals / Section 230 / dating-app litigation**
- boingboing.net / NPR, Mar 22–23, 2018 — Craigslist took its Personals section offline worldwide the day FOSTA-SESTA passed; Craigslist's stated reason: "We can't take such risk without jeopardizing all our other services." [search snippet only, well-corroborated by NPR]
- Doe v. Grindr, 9th Cir., decided Feb 18, 2025 (cert denied Oct 14, 2025) — Section 230 barred state-law defective-design/negligence claims where a minor was matched with adults and later raped; TVPRA claim failed for lack of actual-knowledge/participation by Grindr. [search snippet only, corroborated across EFF, Justia, Eric Goldman's blog]
- Herrick v. Grindr, 2d Cir., 2019 (cert denied) — Section 230 shielded Grindr even where the plaintiff alleged 50+ complaints about an ex using the app to send "hundreds" of men to his home/work via fake profiles; district judge said "I don't find what Grindr did to be acceptable" but ruled Section 230 barred the suit anyway. [search snippet only]
- themarkup.org / 19thnews.org, Dec 16–23, 2025 — Six women sued Match Group (Hinge/Tinder) over a Denver cardiologist convicted of drugging/assaulting 11 women, alleging the company "knew as early as September 2020" and failed to ban him; lawsuit calls the unmatch-before-report design flow "defective." Dec 2025 testing by the Dating Apps Reporting Project found banned users could re-register on Hinge, Plenty of Fish, and OkCupid with the same name/photo/birthday. [search snippet only]
- UK Online Safety Act — as of July 25, 2025, dating apps (Tinder, Bumble, Hinge, Grindr, Feeld) must run "highly effective" age assurance and address romance fraud, harassment, stalking, and grooming risks, with penalties up to 10% of global revenue or £18M. [search snippet only]

- Comparitech/press aggregation, 2025 YTD — 55,604 reports in first nine months of 2025 (up 22% YoY), losses over $1.16B, median loss $2,218 in Q3 2025; ~60% of 2025 romance-scam victims said it started on a social media platform, not a dating app. [search snippet only] — directly relevant: scam origination on non-dating social/marketplace-adjacent platforms is already common.

No source specifically documents a "sentimental object story" vector on a gifting platform like Keepsake — this is an inferred analogy, not a documented case. - No source found directly stating standard marketplace general-liability policies carry a categorical "dating services" exclusion that would apply to Keepsake; this is an inference from how dating-specific insurance products are marketed, not a documented exclusion clause. **Flag for a lawyer/insurance broker**: confirm whether framing Keepsake as facilitating "meetings between strangers" (romantic language especially) could trigger underwriting questions or exclusions in a general marketplace GL policy.

- Nextdoor has repeatedly had to publicly disclaim being a dating platform despite users treating it that way — a cautionary example of a neighborhood-utility product acquiring an unwanted dating reputation organically, not by design choice. ## Suggested mitigations (from the evidence, not legal advice)

- **Copy/positioning**: Never use language resembling "match," "spark," "connection," "who loves it most" in a romantic sense — keep all copy about the object's story, not about people meeting people. Explicitly state in ToS/FAQ "Keepsake is not a dating or social platform" (preempting the Nextdoor problem).
- **Product**: No public profile browsing by other requesters, no visible photos of requesters to the giver beyond what's needed to pick a recipient; strip any feature that lets a giver browse "who else is interested" as if browsing profiles.
- **Moderation**: Adopt Vinted/Depop-style explicit "no romantic/sexual approaches" rule in community guidelines from day one, with a reporting path, rather than adding it reactively after incidents (Vinted's current gap — no automated filter, only after-the-fact reports — is a stated weakness to avoid replicating).
- **Safety**: Recommend/require public-place handoffs; consider partnering with or pointing users to existing local "safe exchange zone" locations (police-station lots) for handoffs, especially cross-gender/first-time exchanges.
- **Legal (flag for counsel)**: Have a lawyer confirm (a) general-liability coverage doesn't carry a "dating/social matching" exclusion given the note-and-choose mechanic, and (b) Section 230 posture given the note/email intermediation step — confirm Keepsake's confirmation-of-email step doesn't create a "matching" function that courts could analogize to Grindr/Match Group design claims.
- **Scam defense**: Since ~60% of 2025 romance-scam victims say the scam started on a social platform (not a dating app), a story-and-note mechanic should have basic anti-scam friction: no off-platform payment requests solicited through notes, and moderation flags for notes containing off-platform contact requests or urgency/crisis language typical of romance-scam scripts.
