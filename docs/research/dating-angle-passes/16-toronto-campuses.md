## Verdict

**University of Toronto St. George is the best first Canadian campus**, primarily on the strength of density-plus-precedent: it packs ~69,900 students and a documented, sustainability-office-run reuse channel (the U of T Free Store, run by Regenesis at 487 Spadina Cres., St. George campus) into a single FSA (M5S) that Keepsake's postcode+radius model was built for — no other Toronto campus offers that combination of scale, a live give-away precedent, and one clean postal boundary. The single best piece of evidence: U of T's own Facilities & Services site documents an active, staffed **Free Store** stocked from move-out donation drives [verified by fetch, fs.utoronto.ca] — this is a near-exact non-digital analog of Keepsake's core loop already running on the exact campus in question, at the exact FSA Keepsake would scope to.

## Risks / objections

- Several headline numbers (residence bed totals for St. George specifically, Facebook group member counts, follower counts, tabling rules for York) rely only on search snippets, not a direct fetch of a primary source — treat as directional, not final, before using in an external-facing deck.
- No verified Canadian precedent exists either way on cease-and-desist risk for a campus-branded gifting app — this is a real unknown, not a cleared risk.
- U of T's actual reuse precedent (Free Store) is a physical give-away model, not a peer-to-peer app — it validates *demand and campus tolerance* for free-stuff redistribution, but is not proof a digital marketplace would be welcomed the same way; recognized-student-group gatekeeping (governingcouncil.utoronto.ca policy) suggests Keepsake would need a campus-club partner to table or promote, not just to operate the website.
- Sidechat's confirmed foothold is at McGill, not any Toronto school — if campus anonymous-social-app precedent matters to the thesis, McGill is the stronger Canadian comparator, not U of T/TMU/York.
- CASL/PIPEDA analysis here is generic secondary-source guidance, not a legal opinion — a real Canadian privacy/anti-spam counsel review is needed before any student-email outreach campaign.
