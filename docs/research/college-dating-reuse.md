# College: where dating, reuse and gifting meet — and what Keepsake can build there — 2026-09-09

Companion to [`dating-angle.md`](./dating-angle.md) (the general answer: face the objects, never
the meeting), [`events-and-press-houston.md`](./events-and-press-houston.md) (which already found
Rice's spring move-out programme and nothing else on campus) and
[`canada-and-status.md`](./canada-and-status.md) (Toronto). Six parallel web passes on 2026-09-09:
the national campus reuse ecosystem, college dating culture and its matching rituals, the
campus-by-campus launch playbook, the Houston campuses one by one, the Toronto campuses one by
one, and an adversarial pass on the legal and safety edges of a college framing. Every claim
carries a source and a confidence tag (*[verified]* fetched and read; *[snippet]* search result or
mirror only; *[unverified]* secondary claim); "not found" is said where it applies.

Before this pass the repository held one row on college: Rice's "Give a Hoot! Donate Your Loot!"
spring move-out collection. Everything below is new.

## 0. The answer

The conjunction is real, and it is **the pass-down, not the pick-up line.**

- **The reuse half is enormous, institutional and on a fixed calendar.** About 200 US schools
  run donate-and-rehouse move-out programmes through one network, and roughly half of the
  country's ~2,057 residential campuses now have one (§1). Single campuses move 60–250 tons in a
  two-to-three-week window each May. Houston has two of these programmes, and one of them (UH)
  sits next to a 24-hour police-lobby "Safe Trade Location" built for exactly the handoff Keepsake
  depends on (§4).
- **The dating half is a ritual appetite, not a swiping appetite.** College students are the most
  burned-out cohort on apps and the least likely to be dating at all (§2). What spreads on a
  campus is a one-shot, near-zero-effort, everyone's-doing-it seasonal ritual: Marriage Pact took
  60% of Stanford undergrads in days and is at 109 schools; Rice's fourth Marriage Pact, January
  2026, had "record-breaking engagement" (§2, §4). Fizz's campus marketplace, live beside its
  "Crush" posts, did 50,000 listings and 150,000 DMs in its first three months (§2).
- **The conjunction that is safe to build is the one Keepsake already is:** a graduating student
  posts the object of their four years with its story; an underclassman writes why it should be
  theirs; the senior chooses. That is the "big/little" gift, the hand-me-down and the
  senior-will tradition (§2) carried by the existing note-and-choose mechanic. It is emotionally
  charged in the way a campus ritual needs to be, and it never has to say "meet."
- **The conjunction that is not safe is the one the brief floated:** a college framing "with a
  meet-people undertone." A campus population contains 17-year-olds, Keepsake has no age gate
  and no accounts, Texas Penal Code §33.021 needs no meeting to have occurred, the SCOPE Act has a
  "willful disregard" prong, and every campus name and mascot is a licensed mark (§5). The
  adversarial pass called this a non-starter as scoped, and nothing in the other five passes
  contradicts it.

**Recommendation.** Run one campus ritual — working name *The Pass-Down* — in the spring 2027
move-out window (UH's programme has historically run 21 April to 12 May), scoped by postcode
radius around 77005 (Rice) and 77204 (UH), seeded through a registered student organisation and
the two student papers, with the copy facing the object and the class year, not the person. Rice
for the ritual, UH for the infrastructure; they are a few miles apart, so one radius can hold both
(check the exact radius against the feed before promising it). Toronto's equivalent is U of T St.
George, FSA M5S, which already runs a physical Free Store stocked from move-out (§6).

## 1. The campus reuse ecosystem, nationally

- **Post-Landfill Action Network (PLAN)**, founded 2013, has worked with ~200 schools; its
  founder estimates about half of the 2,057 US residential colleges now have a donate-and-rehouse
  programme *[verified, Christian Science Monitor, 2026-05-19]*.
- **Volumes per campus, 2024** *[verified, Dropcurb report]*: Tufts 230 tons a year; Boston
  University 113 tons in May 2024; Stanford 69 tons in 2024 and 612 since 2013; UNH 105 tons in
  May against a 25-ton typical month; UT Austin's MoveOutATX 249 tons cumulative. The average
  student generates ~640 lb of waste a year, concentrated in a 2–3-week May window; ~19M US
  college students, ~22% living on campus *[snippet, same report]*.
- **Named programmes** *[snippet unless marked]*: Goodwill "Donate Don't Dump" (University of
  Iowa: 21,158 lb, 486 students, 1,036 items in one window); BU "Goodwill Not Landfill" (2M+ lb
  since 2009, a record 150,000+ lb in May 2024); FSU "Chuck It for Charity" (25,853 lb in a
  season, 14th year); Penn State "Trash to Treasure" (60–68 tons a year, 2002–2019, ~$1M raised,
  ended and replaced by a Goodwill partnership); UC Berkeley ReUSE (furniture exchange 22–31
  May); University of San Diego "Eco Exit"/Torero Thrift (4.3 tons in year one, collects in
  spring, **gives away free to incoming students in fall**, explicitly includes decor
  *[verified]*).
- **The peer-to-peer layer.** Fizz's marketplace: live on 240 college and 60 high-school
  campuses; 50,000 listings and 150,000 DMs March–May 2024; clothing ~25% of listings; no
  payments *[verified, TechCrunch 2024-07-03]*. Rumie (.edu-verified marketplace): 320,000+
  students, 1,078 universities, $450k seed in 2024 *[snippet]*. Facebook "Free & For Sale"
  groups: Berkeley's is cited at both ~10,000 and ~47,000 members (sources conflict); Pitt's at
  12,936 *[snippet]*.
- **Abandoned-property clocks are fast.** Most schools dispose of left items within 24–72 hours
  and charge $50–300 *[verified, Storage Scholars]*. Keepsake's post → notes → choose loop has to
  start *before* the last day, not on it.
- **Startups in the space** are movers and resellers — Storage Scholars (200+ campuses, Shark
  Tank), Dorm Room Movers, Rumie, Campus Swap, TreasureX, Campus Reclaimed *[snippet]*. None is
  story-first; none is free-only. "Bunz U": **not found**.
- **"Senior wills."** A real, century-old tradition of bequeathing objects, traits and advice to
  underclassmen in the school paper or yearbook — **documented at high schools**; a widespread
  college-newspaper version **could not be verified**. Greek "big/little" gift weeks (paddles,
  crafts, "lasting mementos of the bond") are live and emotionally charged *[snippet]*, with no
  count of how often an object with a story moves that way.

**Read.** Keepsake does not have to teach the behaviour; it has to add the story to a behaviour
that already happens in bulk, on a date, next to a tent. The tent's competitor is the bulk sort,
not another app: whatever goes into the Goodwill truck loses its story at the truck.

## 2. What college students want, and which rituals spread

- **Not swiping, and not dating much.** Forbes Health 2024: 79% of Gen Z app users report
  burnout *[snippet; Forbes blocks fetch]*. Survey Center on American Life, 2024-02-09
  *[verified]*: 56% of Gen Z adults had a romantic relationship at any point as teenagers, against
  78% of Boomers and 76% of Gen X. Twenge / Monitoring the Future *[snippet]*: high-school
  seniors who had dated in the past year fell from 80% (2000) to 45% (2023). Hinge's Gen Z report,
  Nov 2025 (~30,000 daters) *[snippet]*: 84% "want new ways to build deeper emotional
  connections." The widely quoted "57% of Gen Z not looking, per Pew" **could not be traced to a
  Pew document** — do not use it.
- **What spreads is a one-shot seasonal ritual with social proof.** Marriage Pact *[verified,
  Stanford Daily and Stanford Magazine]*: two students emailed ~10 lists as a class project in
  2017; 1,000+ responses in 13 hours, 4,100+ (nearly 60% of undergrads) within days; 4,177 at
  Stanford in 2025 and 99% of seniors before graduating; at Duke ~4,500 (two-thirds of undergrads)
  in one week after a viral TikTok; 109 schools and ~629,000 cumulative participants by Nov 2025
  *[snippet, the last figure]*; $5M seed, free to students. Founder: "most people sign up for the
  very fact that their friends are doing it." Datamatch *[verified, Harvard Crimson 2025-02]*:
  ~2,700 Harvard participants in 2025, down 1,400+ after its student-government grant was cut from
  $6,000 to $4,000 — outcomes reported were mostly platonic. Cornell Perfect Match: 5,000+
  registered by 2025 *[snippet]*. Rituals need institutional backing to keep momentum, and they
  produce friendships more reliably than romances.
- **Anonymous reach-outs are a standing campus genre.** Fizz's "Crush" post category sits beside
  its marketplace *[verified]*; campus crush and confession Instagram accounts are everywhere
  (@unccrushes ~500 posts; @collegefessing ~6M followers *[snippet]*); two academic studies of
  Facebook confession pages (one over ~200,000 posts) *[snippet]*. Rice and UH each have a
  "missed connections" account (§4).
- **Students meeting partners through free-stuff channels:** **not found** at the college level.

**Read.** The mechanic students demonstrably opt into is "fill in one thing, once, because
everyone is, and see what comes back." Keepsake's per-item flow is heavier than a survey, so the
ritual needs a window, a name and a campus to be "the thing everyone did in May," and it needs a
student organisation behind it the way Datamatch needs its grant. The romance in the ritual is
the *possibility*, never the promise — Datamatch's own outcomes say so.

*Sources: TechCrunch (Fizz 2022-10-04, Tinder 2014-07-09), Digiday (Yik Yak), Stanford Magazine
(Marriage Pact), Harvard Crimson 2018-02-07 (Datamatch), Dorm Room Fund (Snackpass) — verified
where marked in the pass, otherwise snippet.*

- **Student papers cover launches.** The Rice Thresher ran "'Real life sort of friendship': Nudge
  app launches at Rice" (launched 2025-02-24, built by two sophomores through the Lilie Lab
  Launchpad) *[snippet; ricethresher.org returns 403 to fetch]*. The Daily Cougar equivalent:
  **not found in this pass**. The Varsity covers student startups through U of T's Hub and
  Entrepreneurship Week rather than cold pitches *[snippet]*.
- **Tabling needs a sponsor.** The common rule (Villanova, Brown, Tufts, UMD): an outside
  organisation needs a registered student organisation or department sponsor, a member present
  the whole time, and a purpose tied to the sponsor's mission *[snippet]*. Rice: registered
  organisations solicit via the Office of Student Activities, and posters have needed a sponsor
  name, date and contact since the 2024-08-30 policy tightening *[snippet, Thresher]*. UH: outside
  organisations reserve through Conference & Reservation Services with fees, and need a
  registered-organisation or department sponsor (MAPP 01.05.01) *[snippet]*.
- **Nobody scopes a campus by postcode without a login.** Every comparable product uses .edu
  email as its trust and scoping layer. Keepsake's radius-around-a-ZIP, no-account model is
  untested territory *[not found]* — and it means Keepsake's "campus" is a neighbourhood that
  includes the campus, which is honest and should be said that way.
- **What failed.** Yik Yak: 70+ women's and civil-rights groups petitioned the Department of
  Education in 2015 over threats; bans at half a dozen campuses; shut 2017, IP sold for $1M.
  Fizz: a 2021 database exposure let three students deanonymise posts; Fizz threatened them
  legally before the EFF stepped in *[verified, Stanford Daily 2022-11-01]*. Sidechat: used to
  doxx a Jewish student at Columbia; Harvard's president called it "poisonous" in April 2024
  testimony; blocked on UNC wifi *[snippet]*. Every failure is anonymity plus no moderation;
  Keepsake has a verified email behind every request and a human queue, which is the opposite
  design — but a solo operator has no moderation staff if a campus wave arrives.

## 5. The edges, adversarially

- **Minors.** A share of first-year students are 17 at matriculation (a precise four-year figure
  was **not found**; NCES Digest Table 303.40 is the place to look). Texas contracts with minors
  are voidable at the minor's election *[snippet]*. **Texas Penal Code §33.021(c)** makes online
  solicitation of a minor to meet, with sexual intent, a felony, and "it is not a defense that the
  meeting did not occur" *[snippet, Justia]*. The **SCOPE Act (HB 18)**: the age-verification and
  parental-consent provisions took effect 2024-09-01 and remain in force; the content-filtering
  provisions remain enjoined after a 2–1 Fifth Circuit affirmance in July 2026 *[verified,
  Wikipedia, dated 2026-07-25]*. "Known minor" means actual knowledge *or willful disregard*
  *[snippet, FPF]* — a no-account service is hard to hold to actual knowledge; a service marketing
  itself at a campus is easier to hold to willful disregard. `legal-considerations.md` already
  flags that neither the terms nor the site mention age; a college pass makes that the first
  thing to fix.
- **Marks.** Rice requires a licence for any use of its name or marks and bars unaffiliated
  parties from using them in commerce or advertising *[snippet, brand.rice.edu]*; Texas A&M sent a
  cease-and-desist to "TexAM University" in 2025 over name confusion alone *[snippet]*. No "Rice
  edition," no owls, no cougars, no campus colours without clearance. Canada: no campus-app
  cease-and-desist precedent found either way (§6).
- **Scams.** FBI IC3 2025: 23,159 romance and confidence-fraud complaints, $929.3M, up 38%;
  75,000+ sextortion submissions, 11,000 from people under 20 *[snippet]*. The note-and-email
  format as a vector is an inference, not a documented case.
- **Title IX / Clery** case naming an outside platform: **not found**.
- **Insurance.** No underwriting source names "college-targeted" as a rating factor
  *[not found]*; the December 2025 Match Group suit shows the plaintiffs' theory being tested
  *[snippet]*. Ask the broker (#142) the same question `dating-angle.md` §2.9 asks, plus "campus."

**A lawyer must check** (not legal advice): whether campus-targeted marketing reaches the SCOPE
Act's willful-disregard prong for a no-account site; what an age affirmation does and does not
buy; whether the GL quote excludes facilitated in-person meetings, and whether "campus" changes
that; and whether any campus copy could be read as facilitating solicitation under §33.021 —
which is the reason the copy never says "meet."

## 7. What to build: *The Pass-Down*, spring 2027

**The ritual.** In the last three weeks of April 2027, graduating students post the objects of
their four years — the lamp from the first dorm, the mug from the all-nighter, the poster, the
plant — with the story; underclassmen say why it should be theirs; the senior chooses; the handoff
happens on campus or at the UHPD lobby. Copy faces the object and the class year: *Give it to the
next four years. Say where it's been.*

**Why this shape and not another.**
- It rides the only institutional window Houston has (UH 21 Apr–12 May historical; Rice Give a
  Hoot in spring) and beats the 24–72-hour abandoned-property clock by starting early (§1).
- It is a single seasonal opt-in with a name, which is the shape that spreads on a campus (§2).
- It carries the emotional charge of the big/little gift and the senior will without the word
  "meet" (§2, §5).
- It gives `bulk-upload.md`'s estate flow a second customer: a graduating senior with 20 objects
  is an estate in miniature.

**Sequence, cheapest first.**
1. **Fix the age line** in the terms and the FAQ before any campus copy exists (§5).
2. **One sponsor.** A registered student organisation at Rice (EcoReps is the obvious fit, given
   its clothing swap) and the UH Office of Sustainability's programme contact (§4). Ask for a
   table at the move-out tent and a mention in the programme's own posts; do not table alone.
3. **Two papers.** Pitch the Thresher the way Nudge was covered — a student-built-adjacent launch
   story with the ritual as the hook — and The Daily Cougar with the safe-trade-lobby angle.
   Datamatch's tactic of co-owning with a campus publication (§3) is worth trying with either.
4. **One radius.** A Keepsake link with the postcode pre-filled to 77005, radius wide enough to
   cover 77204; check the boundary against the feed and say "near Rice and UH," never "Rice."
5. **Yik Yak's email.** Student organisations, residential colleges, Greek chapters — the
   zero-dollar seed that took Yik Yak from 30 to 300 schools (§3).
6. **Fall 2027**: whatever seniors could not place by May is the incoming class's free store,
   the USD model (§1), and the second half of the ritual.

**Never.** "Meet," "match," "crush" (Fizz's word, and the adversary's example), a campus name in
Keepsake's own branding, a mascot, requester photos, any browse of other requesters, or any copy
that implies who is on the other end of the note is the point.

**Measure.** The two numbers `product-decisions.md` already tracks — `request_confirmed` against
`request_sent`, and unconfirmed requests swept — plus, for this ritual only, listings posted in
the window with a class-year mention in the story, and handoffs the giver reports at the UHPD
lobby.

## 8. What would change the answer

- **Toward more:** a Rice or UH student organisation asks to run it rather than merely to host a
  table; the Thresher covers it; the spring 2027 window shows a request rate per listing above the
  Houston baseline.
- **Toward less:** the lawyer's answer on SCOPE Act willful disregard or the age line; a
  cease-and-desist from either university over a campus reference; a single request note that
  reads as solicitation before the terms line exists; the broker excluding campus-adjacent
  meetups.
- **Not a trigger:** Fizz or Rumie adding a "free" tab. Neither is story-first, and neither lets
  the giver choose.
