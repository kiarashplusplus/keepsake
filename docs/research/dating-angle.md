# The dating angle — what the evidence supports, and what it warns against — 2026-09-09

Companion to [`../market-research.md`](../market-research.md) (competitors),
[`demand-houston.md`](./demand-houston.md) (who asks) and

## 0. The answer

There is a dating angle, and it is not "Keepsake as a place to meet people." It is **Keepsake as
where the objects of a relationship go next** — the gifts an ex left behind, the veil and the
guest book after the wedding (or the divorce), the "something borrowed" a stranger lends the next
bride — with the existing mechanic (a stranger writes why they would love this, and you choose)
framed for what it already is: the one part of a courtship ritual that a gifting site can carry
safely.

Three tiers came out of the evidence, in decreasing strength:

1. **Love objects as supply and as press hook — strong.** A 1,000-person US survey has 65% of
   adults still holding an item from an ex, half for five years or more, and 78% saying they will
   get rid of it "someday" (§2.1). Houston's own TV stations have run the "get rid of your ex's
   stuff" story twice (§2.8). On the wedding side, a 2022 Facebook giveaway reproduced Keepsake's
   exact mechanic — 70 brides wrote why they wanted one dress, the owner chose — and became a
   group that moved 200+ dresses; Buy Nothing counts 100+ brides in free dresses; a 2026 survey of
   800 engaged couples has 61% wanting "meaningful pieces with stories" (§2.2).
2. **The note-and-choose mechanic as courtship, in copy — moderate.** Givers reliably
   underestimate how much a sentimental gift or a small kindness to a stranger will mean (four
   studies, §2.3); a product's effortful signal (a Hinge comment, a Bumble first line) measurably
   outperforms a tap (§2.3). This licenses copy like "notes are read by a person, not swiped" and
   "you get to choose." It does not license the word "match."
3. **An explicit dating or flirting frame — net negative.** Every marketplace with no dating
   intent (Vinted, Depop, Poshmark, Nextdoor) has had to write an anti-flirting rule after the
   fact; the object-and-hobby swipe apps are a graveyard; Craigslist dropped Personals the day
   FOSTA-SESTA passed rather than carry the exposure (§2.7, §2.9). A one-person sole
   proprietorship with no cover bound (`product-decisions.md` §1) should not be the next test case.

The one genuinely new thing this pass found is that **the wedding vertical already runs an
unbranded Keepsake** — Shared Dream Dresses and Buy Nothing weddings are note-and-choose gifting
of love objects, at scale, on Facebook. That is a wedge, and it is the same wedge as "the objects
of a relationship," not a separate one.

## 2. Findings by angle

### 2.1 What an ex left behind

- **Neighbor.com / Pollfish survey, 1,000 US adults, 2022-02-14** *[verified via studyfinds.com
  mirror; the neighbor.com original blocked the fetch]*: 65% still have an item from an ex; 44%
  keep it for fond memories; the most-kept items are love letters and cards (50%), photos (46%)
  and jewelry (43%); nearly half have kept the item five years or more; 78% say they will
  eventually throw it out; men keep items at a higher rate (68% vs 62%) but women keep them longer
  (50% vs 32% past five years). Letters, photos and jewelry are Keepsake's first, second and sixth
  categories exactly.
- **Museum of Broken Relationships.** ~3,500 donated objects *[verified, Wikipedia]*; Zagreb drew
  111,798 visitors in 2019 *[snippet]*; a second permanent branch opened in Chiang Mai on
  2024-11-08 and a Vienna touring show is listed for 2026-09-14 to 2026-10-16 *[snippet]*. The
  cautionary half: the Los Angeles branch, opened June 2016, closed 2017-11-22 after about 18
  months *[snippet]*. The founder describes donation as a "cathartic ritual" *[snippet]* — the
  donor's motive is release, not the object's next life.
- **Never Liked It Anyway** — "the eBay for breakups," launched 2011, took a 10% cut for the
  American Heart Association. Every substantive press hit is 2011–2012; the site is still up and
  looks dormant *[verified]*. No other breakup marketplace, live or dead, was found. Nobody has
  shown that people *transact* around ex-objects at scale, even for free.
- **Stunts that reliably earn press every February** *[snippet, corroborated across outlets]*:
  zoo "name a cockroach after your ex" fundraisers (Bronx Zoo: 56,000 roaches named over a decade
  *[verified, Bronx Times 2025]*; Brookfield, Birmingham, San Antonio, El Paso all live in 2026);
  P.F. Chang's "Love Lost, DUMPlings Found," 2024-02-07, 35,000+ texts and 3,500+ redemptions;
  Tinder India's "Ex-press Disposal Truck," mid-2025. None had a Houston leg; the *format* is what
  transfers (§2.8).
- **Not found:** a Reddit "what to do with my ex's stuff" megathread; any indexed "my ex gave me
  this" listing on Buy Nothing, Vinted or Depop; TikTok engagement numbers for the "ripping up my
  ex's stuff" genre, which exists but is not measurable from outside.

**Read.** The demand is latent and self-acknowledged ("someday"), the press appetite is proven,
and the recipient side is unproven — the museum works because nobody takes the object home. On
Keepsake the object is used, so the story has to make it wanted, not merely released. That argues
for a shelf and a week, not a category, and for copy that faces the object's next life rather than
the ex.

### 2.2 The objects of a wedding — and of the next one

- **Shared Dream Dresses** *[NPR 2022-07-22; the npr.org page returned 503, the story is
  corroborated verbatim across ~10 affiliate reposts]*: Gwendolyn Stulgis gave her wedding dress
  away on Facebook in 2022, received 70+ messages from brides saying what it would mean to them,
  chose one, and turned the response into a group that had exchanged 200+ dresses by the time NPR
  wrote it up. This is Keepsake's mechanic — photo, story, notes, giver's choice — run by hand.
- **The Buy Nothing Wedding** *[verified, buynothingproject.org, dated 2026-08-19]*: "more than
  100 brides wore Buy Nothing wedding dresses" over about two years, "nearly $350,000" kept out of
  credit-card debt, "3 tons" out of landfill; one bride borrowed a stranger's veil "because she
  wanted it to have a second life."
- **Zola × Poshmark "One-of-One Wedding" report, 800+ engaged couples, 2026-04-08** *[verified]*:
  61% seek "meaningful pieces with stories"; 34% plan to use family heirlooms; 50% incorporate
  vintage or secondhand jewelry; 71% choose vintage "to create one-of-a-kind celebrations"; only
  12% describe their ring as vintage — an aspiration gap Keepsake cannot fill with rings (they are
  outside the free-gifting frame) but can with everything around the ring.
- **The unwanted-dress channel is large and paid.** Brides for a Cause: 50,000+ dresses since
  2012, $4M+ raised, a record $1M in 2025 *[verified]*. Stillwhite: 103,350+ gowns listed, sellers
  paid $20M+ *[snippet]*. Something Borrowed, Portland OR, is a free bridal lending library
  *[snippet]*. Keepsake's difference is free, local and story-first — the giver picks who wears it.
- **Love letters** are the most-kept ex item (50%, §2.1) and an active Etsy/eBay category
  *[snippet]*; More Love Letters (Hannah Brencher, 2011) claims 125,000+ letters sent, but as
  kindness, not romance *[snippet]*. **Do not conflate** with Letters to Strangers, a youth
  mental-health nonprofit *[verified]*.
- **Not found:** volume data on rings after divorce; a "furnished our first apartment from Buy
  Nothing" trend at measurable scale (Apartment Therapy's secondhand home tours exist *[snippet]*);
  anything Houston-specific.

**Read.** This is the strongest angle in the pass because it is not an analogy: strangers already
write notes to win a dress, and owners already choose. The addressable objects are the dress,
veil, guest book, cake topper, ring box, letters and the first-home furniture — never the ring.
The divorce half of it is real (Brides for a Cause's supply is partly that) and sensitive in a
public, story-forward format; the copy should face the next bride, not the marriage.

### 2.3 The note and the choice, as courtship

The core mechanic — a stranger says why they would love this, and you pick — resembles courtship.
The literature does not test that mechanic, but it does say three things that bear on it.

- **Givers underestimate sentimental value.** Givi & Galak 2017, *Journal of Consumer Psychology*
  27(4): givers avoid sentimental gifts out of fear of getting it wrong, more than recipients would
  want *[snippet; publisher 403]*. Flynn & Adams 2009, *JESP* 45(2): price predicts appreciation
  in givers' minds, not recipients' *[snippet]*. Zhang & Epley 2012, *JEP: General* 141(4):
  thoughtfulness raises felt connection only when it is made legible *[snippet]*. Kumar & Epley
  2018 (*Psych Science*) and 2022 (*JEP: General*): givers of small unprompted kindnesses to
  strangers consistently underestimate the recipient's gratitude *[snippet]*. Together: the
  giver's hesitation ("will a stranger even want this?") is the documented bias, and reading the
  notes is the documented cure — the notes make the thoughtfulness legible.
- **Effortful signals outperform taps, in product data.** Hinge newsroom, 2025-01-15
  *[verified]*: likes on text prompts were 47% more likely to lead to a date than likes on photos;
  a reply within 24 hours makes a date 72% more likely *[verified]*. Bumble's one-word-opener
  friction raised mutual-chat rates, more than twice as much for women *[verified, GMA]*. Tinder
  Super Like: marketing says 3×; an independent 100-vs-100 test found 17% vs 11%, and heavy
  senders under-perform — effort signals cheapen when overused *[snippet]*.
- **"Giver's choice" is already the norm on Buy Nothing**, where requests that say *why* are
  favoured over first-come *[snippet, docs.buynothingproject.org; nextcity.org]*.
- **Handle with care.** Costly-signalling theory (Zahavi, Miller) is contested even among
  biologists — cite as a lens. Dunn, Aknin & Norton 2008 on prosocial spending has a registered
  replication that did not reproduce the primary effect *[snippet, PMC9451075]* — do not cite it
  clean. Loneliness numbers (Surgeon General 2023 ~50%; Gallup 2025 daily loneliness 20%, men
  15–34 at 25%; Cigna 2025 57%, Gen Z 67%) are all *[snippet]*; the Cigna PDF could not be read.

**Read.** Enough to write "people almost always underestimate how much a sentimental object will
mean to someone else — read the notes" on the giver's screen, and to frame the request note as
thirty seconds of real thought. Not enough to say the mechanic makes anyone fall in love, and no
reason to say it.

### 2.4 Objects as icebreakers

- Aron et al. 1997 *[verified]*: escalating reciprocal self-disclosure produces more closeness
  than small talk. eNeuro 2021 *[verified]*: sharing happy stories with strangers raises rated
  closeness. Belk 1988 ("we are what we have") and Csikszentmihalyi & Rochberg-Halton 1981 (80
  Chicago families; household objects carry symbolic and emotional weight) *[snippet]*.
- Products that lead with text or objects instead of faces: Lex (text-first personals, 2019/2020;
  "millions of messages a month" by 2025 *[snippet]*); Shelf'd, "connect through books, not
  looks," a bookshelf as the profile *[verified]*. Missed Connections are resurging on Reddit and
  TikTok as "an analogue alternative to dating apps" *[verified, Yahoo, 2025-07]*.
- **Not found:** any controlled study of an object as an icebreaker between strangers; a named
  TikTok "most sentimental object" format with numbers; a story-first mechanic at Feeld.

**Read.** Two adjacent literatures, never fused. This is the evidentiary base for the mandatory
story (`packages/contracts`: min 20 chars) and for "connect through objects, not photos" as a
line — not for a positioning.

### 2.5 The post-app shift

- CBS News 2024-08-03 *[verified]*: dating-app downloads down 16% 2020→2023; Match paying users
  down 5% to 15.2M (Q4 2023), Tinder down 8% to 10M, Hinge up 33% to 1.4M; Lunge Run Club went
  from 20–30 to 400–500 people an event; Brickell Run Club to 1,600 a week across 8 clubs.
- Eventbrite "online to offline" report *[verified]*: 1.5M+ searches for dating and singles
  events May 2023–Apr 2024; 82% want "a mutually enjoyable activity," not a formal date; 69% of
  Millennials prefer in-person for authenticity; 33% think they will meet someone at a hobby class.
- *[snippet, several key pages 403]*: Bloomberg 2024-05-07, sixth straight quarterly Tinder
  decline; Ofcom 2024, UK dating-app use down 16%; Forbes Health 2025, 78% report burnout; Hinge's
  "One More Hour," $1M for ~100 social events, March 2025; Strava 2025, 1 in 5 respondents had
  dated someone from a run club; Timeleft 150,000 diners a month, 200+ cities, ~6,500 dinners a
  week, €18M ARR; Axios 2026-08-17, "meet-cute" events up 117% in titles and 128% in attendance.
- Thrifting as a date: a TikTok "thrift date challenge" exists *[snippet, no numbers]*; ThredUp
  2025, 68%+ of Gen Z and Millennials bought secondhand apparel in 2024 *[snippet]*; EstateSales.net
  reports younger buyers, with no dating link *[snippet]*. Houston: Antique Gallery of Houston
  (85,000 sq ft, 250+ vendors), a Singles Mixer at HMNS in August 2025, MFAH's adult "Night at the
  MFAH" *[snippet]*. **Not found:** any dating-app × thrift-brand partnership; Houston press on
  thrifting as a date.

**Read.** The culture wants a pretext to be in a room with strangers that is not explicitly
dating. A free feed you browse without an account and a handoff in a public place is that shape.
Say "a reason to meet a neighbour," never "a date."

### 2.6 Browsing strangers' stories

- Humans of New York ~30M followers *[verified, Wikipedia]*; PostSecret, weekly since 2005, ~1M
  visits a month *[snippet]*; Craigslist Missed Connections at its peak "6.5 million hits, 14,000
  postings in one January," read mostly by people who never post *[snippet]*; r/relationship_advice
  15.8M members *[snippet]*; Zillow surfing (unique visitors +21% YoY in Nov 2020; an SNL sketch)
  *[snippet]*; Facebook Marketplace ~16% browse-only *[unverified; marketing aggregator]*.
- Estate sales as intimacy with strangers: Longreads "The Magic of Estate Sales" 2019-05-06 ("to
  commune with the departed"); D Magazine "Love Among the Estate Sales," Jan 2022 *[snippet]*.
- US Census 2024 release *[verified]*: one-person households rose from ~18% (1970) to ~29%
  (2022). Harris County ~31% *[snippet, aggregator]*. Sandstrom & Dunn 2014, *PSPB*: weak-tie
  interactions are ~60% of daily social contact and independently raise belonging *[snippet;
  publisher 403]*.

**Read.** People already browse strangers' lives for their own sake, and low-stakes contact with
strangers measurably helps. Nothing ties either to romance. This is the case for marketing the
feed as a thing to read, and the handoff as a weak tie — no more.

### 2.7 Meet-cutes, and the graveyard

- **Meet-cutes exist and are not data.** Fox News 2018-10-18 *[verified]*: an Indiana DJ posted a
  joke "free to a good home" listing of himself on Marketplace and got engaged to a woman it
  reached. Good News Network 2023-04-02 *[verified]*: a Nextdoor bargain-hunter met a neighbour of
  40 years. Fashion Journal 2021-10-13 *[verified]*: a buyer asked a seller for a drink after a
  coffee-table pickup. Chip Chick 2025-09: barstools → boyfriend *[snippet]*. **Not found:** a
  classifieds/marketplace category in the Stanford HCMST or Pew "how couples meet" data; any NYT
  Vows origin story; the "Bunz dating" and "Olio marriages" claims, which could not be verified —
  Bunz's own Friending Zone was explicitly platonic *[verified, BetaKit 2017, Vice 2016]*.
- **The object-and-hobby dating apps died.** Ship (Betches × Match, 2019) "no longer available";
  Twine and Loveflutter (2013–14) dormant; Tastebuds announced shutdown 2023-04-18, gone by 2025;
  Sweatt stalled; Kippo relaunched *[all snippet]*. Survivors are async, no-swipe, story-led: Lex,
  PostSecret, Olio (9M+ downloads, a forum and events; 76% self-report better mental health
  *[snippet, company-reported]*), Timeleft. Bunz's own FAQ *[verified]*: independent, and it has
  said it would shut by 2026-09-01 without 1,000 daily actives in one city — a token pivot in
  2018–19 cost it the community. **Not found:** any live 2025–26 startup pitching "meet people
  through the things you give away."

**Read.** A swipe with a gimmick is not a category. Gifting builds community without ever
claiming to be dating (Olio), and the moment "who gets the item" becomes a contest between people
with real names and emails, it is a beauty contest with a liability tail.

- **KPRC / Click2Houston, 2020-02-08** *[verified]*: covered Torchy's Tacos' "Salty Singles Day"

- **Click2Houston, 2025-05-23** *[verified]*: Bill Spencer's piece on Houston Freecycle, Buy

- Weak or unverified hooks: "Red Tuesday" traces to one UK dating site's 2015 member survey; "Divorce

### 2.9 The adversary

- **Non-dating marketplaces wrote anti-flirting rules after the fact.** SW Londoner, 2026-02-04
  *[verified]*: Vinted sellers organising over sexual harassment from buyers; seller Natalia Perry:
  "It's a selling platform not a dating website"; Vinted has no automated filter, only
  after-the-fact reporting. Vinted's "zero-tolerance" safety page and Depop's "Sexually
  Inappropriate Content & Behaviour Policy" (bans "sexualised or flirtatious comments or
  approaches") *[snippet]*. Poshmark and eBay sellers in some categories report threats that
  reference their address *[snippet]*. Nextdoor has publicly said it is not a dating site
  *[unverified primary; several secondary]*. Buy Nothing harassment press: **not found**.
- **The legal tail.** Craigslist took Personals down worldwide on 2018-03-22/23, the day
  FOSTA-SESTA passed: "we can't take such risk without jeopardizing all our other services"
  *[snippet, NPR]*. *Doe v. Grindr*, 9th Cir. 2025-02-18, cert denied 2025-10-14 (§230 barred
  design claims) and *Herrick v. Grindr*, 2d Cir. 2019 *[snippet]* — `legal-next-actions-research.md`
  already cites the former. Six women sued Match Group in December 2025 over a Denver cardiologist
  the company allegedly knew about since September 2020; a reporting project found banned users
  re-registering on Hinge, POF and OkCupid *[snippet]*. UK Online Safety Act from 2025-07-25 puts
  dating apps under "highly effective" age assurance — consistent with the UK block already in
  place.
- **Scams.** FTC 2024: ~34,377 romance-scam reports (loss figures vary by source, $697M–$1.14B);
  the first nine months of 2025: 55,604 reports, up 22%, >$1.16B, and ~60% of victims say it
  started on social media rather than a dating app *[snippet]*. A "sentimental story" as a
  manipulation vector is an inference — no documented case on a gifting platform.
- **Handoffs.** "Robbery by appointment" got Oakland PD a dedicated unit; a rival company's count of
  112 Craigslist-linked deaths in a year is an interested party's number *[snippet]*. Police-lot
  safe-exchange zones began in Boca Raton in 2014 and are common *[snippet]*; a Houston/Harris
  County count: **not found** — a direct HPD/HCSO check is needed.
- **Insurance.** Dating-service guidance says many cyber/internet liability policies exclude
  bodily injury from an assault arising from a facilitated date without an endorsement *[snippet,
  TRUiC]*. Whether a marketplace GL policy carries a "dating services" exclusion: no source —
  **ask the broker** while binding #142, in those words.

## 3. What to do with it

**Positioning line.** *Keepsake is where the things love leaves behind go next.* It faces the
object, it is true of breakups and weddings alike, and it never says "meet."

**Build (small).**
- Two seasonal shelves on the existing aspect-shelf mechanism (`market-research.md` §1):
  **"What love left behind"** for the week before Valentine's, and **"Something borrowed"** for
  wedding season (May–June and September–October). Both are story-tagged, not category-tagged.
- A giver-side line where the notes are read: *People almost always underestimate how much a
  sentimental object will mean to someone else. Read the notes.* (§2.3.)
- A requester-side line above the note: *This is read by the person letting it go, not swiped.
  Say what it would mean to you.* (§2.3.)
- A line in the terms and the FAQ: *Keepsake is not a dating or social platform; romantic or
  sexual approaches through a request are a reason to hide the request and ban the sender.*
  (§2.9 — Vinted and Depop wrote this after the harm; write it before.)
- Public-place handoff copy that names safe-exchange zones once the Houston list exists (§2.9).

**Never.** "Match," "spark," "connection," "who loves it most," or any browse of other
requesters. No requester photos. No feature that turns "who gets it" into a contest between
people rather than between notes.

**Say (press).** Two hooks with in-market precedent: the week before 14 February ("the free,
no-cash alternative to donating your ex's stuff — someone nearby just comes and gets it," pitched to
ABC13 and KPRC, who have run the format) and May, when KPRC ran the free-exchange-groups piece. A
"Something Borrowed, Houston" pitch to r/weddingplanning and the renamed ex-Buy-Nothing groups for
wedding season. Meet-cute anecdotes (§2.7) are colour, never a claim.

## 4. What would change the answer

- **Toward more:** a Houston meet-cute that actually happens through a Keepsake handoff and the
  people involved want it told; or the wedding shelf producing a measurable share of requests with
  notes (the `request_confirmed` number `product-decisions.md` already tracks).
- **Toward less:** a single harassment report through a request note before the terms line
  exists; the broker's answer on a dating-services exclusion; or the SCOPE Act / minor-age
  question (`legal-considerations.md` "age") being answered in a way that makes any
  meeting-adjacent copy untenable.
- **Not a trigger:** a competitor adding a social layer. Olio and Karrot both did; neither claims
  dating, and both are the survivors.
