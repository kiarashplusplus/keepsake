**Read for Keepsake:** both systems explicitly forbid "sale of goods/services," but a Keepsake
table is a **free** giveaway with no sale and no fee collected — the "no solicitation/sales" clause
is about commerce, not about handing over free keepsakes, so this should clear the bar; frame the
booking request as a free public program ("bring a keepsake, tell its story, give it away"), not a
market. HPL additionally bans "required donations," so a suggested-donation framing must be
avoided — keep it strictly $0. Neither policy names a hard nonprofit-status requirement, which
matters because Keepsake is not a nonprofit.

### Five pitch angles

1. **"No money, no accounts, the story travels with the object."** Keepsake has no listing fees,
   no login wall to browse, and every item carries the story of who had it and why they're letting
   it go — the opposite of an anonymous marketplace grid. Good fit for Hello Houston's
   human-interest format and Houstonia's "unique hook" bar.
2. **The estate angle.** Every estate-sale and senior-move-manager source in §3 says the same
   thing: sentimental items nobody will buy end up donated or dumped after a sale. Keepsake is
   the one venue built for exactly that "won't sell, won't dump" pile — a natural Community
   Impact or Houston Landing accountability/quality-of-life story about what happens to a life's
   belongings after a move or death.
3. **The anti-Buy-Nothing-app timing.** Per `seeding-and-distribution.md` §1, Houston's Buy
   Nothing groups went through late-2025 trademark takedowns and are, per §3, openly annoyed at
   the Buy Nothing app's monetization — a warm, primed audience for a no-app, no-account
   alternative right now. Good fit for a consumer-desk story (Bill Spencer at KPRC already covers
   this exact beat) or ABC13's Action 13.
4. **The library-hosted, permit-free pop-up.** A "bring a keepsake, tell its story, give it away"
   table at a free HCPL Repair Café event is a visual, photographable local scene — a fit for
   CultureMap's lifestyle coverage or a Community Impact neighborhood photo story, and it doubles
   as a real seeding event, not a staged one.
5. **A solo operator in Houston building something small and honest.** Houston Landing and
   Houston Press both lean into people-centered, non-corporate storytelling; a founder-profile
   pitch ("one person, no VC, no data harvesting, free gifts only") fits their editorial voice
   better than a product-launch pitch would.
