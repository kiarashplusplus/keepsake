# Keepsake — Cold Outreach Kit

Prepared 2026-09-09 for Kiarash. Everything here matches what Keepsake actually is today: free,
no accounts, no app, no chat feature, no pickup service, no tax receipts, no money changing
hands. If a message here ever drifts from that, fix the message, not the product's description
of itself.

---

## 1. Partner one-pager

*(also usable as a standalone handout or the body of a longer email)*

### Keepsake, for people who see keepsakes every day

Keepsake (keepsake.ing) is a free website where someone photographs a sentimental object they're
ready to let go of, writes its story in a few sentences, and posts it. Anyone nearby can browse
by postcode, and someone who loves it leaves a note and their email. The giver picks who gets it
and hands it over in person, the way you'd hand something to a neighbor. There's no signup, no
price, no bidding, and no money anywhere in it. It exists for the pile everyone in this line of
work knows: too full of memory to sell, too good to throw away, and nowhere obvious to put it.

**What we're asking of a partner.** Either photograph and post a few of the "won't sell,
shouldn't be trashed" pieces you already come across, or simply tell a client, customer, or
visitor that Keepsake exists. That's it. No integration, no paperwork, no ongoing commitment.

**What a partner gets.** A dignified place to put things that would otherwise get thrown out, a
public page you can point clients to, and the good feeling of knowing where something ended up.
Nothing else.

**What a partner doesn't get.** No fee, no referral payment, no data, no tax receipt (Keepsake
isn't a charity and this isn't a donation), no pickup or logistics help, and no obligation to
post anything on a schedule.

Questions: hello@keepsake.ing. Kiarash runs it, in Houston, alone.

### Objections and answers

**"Why would this be free? What's the catch?"**
There isn't a business model behind it, it's a personal project I run because I think these
things deserve somewhere better than a dumpster. Nobody pays, nobody gets paid, and there's no
ad on the page trying to sell you something else.

**"Who's liable if something goes wrong when they meet up?"**
The two people meeting are responsible for that meetup, the same as if they'd found each other on
Craigslist or a Buy Nothing group. I'm not in the middle of the handoff and don't guarantee how it
goes. The site's terms spell that out, and whoever posts an item agrees to it.

**"What if nobody claims it?"**
Then it stays listed, visible to anyone nearby, same as before you posted it. There's no cost to
leaving it up and no expiration pressure. You can also take it down any time and it doesn't come
back to you asking why.

## 4. Partner FAQ

**1. Who is the "giver" on a Keepsake listing?**
Whoever posts the item and will personally hand it over. That has to be someone who actually owns
the object and has the right to give it away. A business can post items that are legitimately
theirs to place, for example unsold estate-sale leftovers a company already has authority to
donate or dispose of, but nobody should post something that's still a client's or a family
member's belonging on their behalf.

**2. What happens to the email address someone leaves?**
If you're the one posting, the give flow asks for an email, and it is only used to notify you
when someone asks for the item and to recover your listings if you lose the device you posted from. If you're the one requesting an item,
the email you leave lets the giver reply and pick you, and that's the whole use of it. No mailing
list, no marketing.

**3. What if an heir or family member shows up later wanting an item back?**
Talk to the family first. If a listing genuinely shouldn't have gone up, tell us and we'll take it
down on request. We'd rather remove something than leave a family dispute sitting in public.

**4. Do we get a tax receipt for anything donated through Keepsake?**
No. Keepsake isn't a charity and items go directly to another individual, not to an organization,
so nothing posted through it is a tax-deductible donation.

**5. How does meeting up work? Is it safe?**
The giver and the person who claimed the item arrange it directly, in a public, well-lit spot,
ideally with a friend along or someone else told the time and place. Leaving something on a
porch, with a neighbor, or at a front desk counts too if neither person wants to meet face to
face.

**6. What if someone doesn't show up?**
Keepsake doesn't manage scheduling or step in when that happens. The two people work it out
between themselves, same as any informal handoff.

**7. What gets rejected by moderation?**
Weapons, drugs, hazardous materials, live animals, anything requiring a licensed transfer,
recalled items, certain older cribs and children's products with lead risk, anything sexually
explicit or hateful, and anything with contact information written into the title or story.
Borderline cases are held for a person to review rather than published automatically.

**8. Can a partner organization post on behalf of a client or donor?**
Only for items that are already the organization's to give, per the answer to question 1.
Posting on behalf of someone who still owns the item isn't allowed under the site's terms.

**9. Is there any cost, contract, or ongoing commitment?**
No. There's nothing to sign, nothing to pay, and no schedule to keep. Post once, post often, or
just tell people it exists.

**10. Who do we contact with a problem or a question?**
hello@keepsake.ing. One person, Kiarash, reads that inbox and answers it himself.
