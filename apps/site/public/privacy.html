Privacy

Last updated September 2026

Keepsake doesn't have accounts, so there isn't much of you to collect in the first place. Here's exactly what we hold, and why.

Who runs this

Keepsake is run by one person, Kiarash, not a company. He's the controller for the purposes of this policy — the one responsible for what's described here, and the one you'd contact or complain to.

No accounts, just a device key

When you post your first keepsake, your browser generates a private token and stores it in your device's local storage. That token is your identity on Keepsake — there's no username, password, or profile. If you clear your browser storage or switch devices without saving an email, we have no way to prove a listing is yours again.

An optional email

You can add an email address when you give something. We store it only to notify you when someone asks for your keepsake and to help you recover access to your dashboard if you lose your device key. We never use it for marketing, and it's never shown publicly on the site.

It is shared privately, though, in one case: if you choose a request, we send your email address to that requester directly (in their notification, and as the reply-to address on it) so the two of you can arrange the handoff. This is the one moment either of your contact details becomes visible to the other person — see the next section.

What a requester shares

If you ask for someone's keepsake, we store the note, email, and optional phone number you provide with that request. This contact information is only shown to the giver, and only once they choose you — and if they do, your email is also included in the "you were chosen" notification we send you, the same way the giver's is (see above).

Every request-confirmation email includes a link to withdraw that request. Following it deletes your note, email, and phone number from our records and marks the request withdrawn — no account or login needed, since you never had one. If the giver already chose you before you withdraw, they may already have your contact information by email; withdrawing removes it from our database and the giver's dashboard going forward, but we can't recall an email that's already been delivered.

Photos

Photos you upload are stored on Cloudflare R2. We keep a full-size and a thumbnail version of each one for as long as the listing exists, plus a short grace period after it's removed.

Screening

Every listing's text and photos are checked automatically by AI models run by Cloudflare at the moment you post, to keep the marketplace safe. Some listings are also reviewed by a human — just Kiarash, today — before they go live.

Rate limiting

To stop abuse, we keep a short-lived, hashed record of your IP address combined with the date (for example, sha256("2026-09-05:203.0.113.4")) instead of your raw IP address. We're being precise rather than absolute about what that buys you: the point is minimization, not secrecy. IPv4 addresses are a small enough space (a few billion) that someone who wanted to unmask one specific day's hash could brute-force it by hashing every possible address — this isn't a strong cryptographic guarantee against a motivated adversary. What it does do: we never handle or store your address in the clear at all, and the hash is scoped to a single day, so two days' records can't be joined into a history of your address the way a raw IP log could be.

Your choice, before anything loads

The first time you arrive, Keepsake asks you one question, and until you answer it nothing optional runs. Rejecting everything is one click, in the same place and the same size as accepting. There are three groups:

Strictly necessary. Not a choice, because without it there is no site: the device key that proves a listing is yours, your light/dark preference, and the record of this very decision. All of it stays in your browser's local storage. None of it goes to anyone else.

Analytics. Off unless you say yes. If you say yes, we load Cloudflare Web Analytics from static.cloudflareinsights.com. It counts page views without cookies and without following you to other sites. Cloudflare is the recipient.

Advertising. Off unless you say yes. If you say yes, we load the Google Ads tag from www.googletagmanager.com so we can tell which advert brought you here. Google is the recipient, and it may set cookies on this site. We never send it your photos, your story text, your email, or anything a requester wrote to you.

One more thing worth saying plainly: if we haven't switched a tool on for a particular build, it doesn't load even when you've said yes. Consent is the ceiling, not the trigger.

Changing your mind

"Privacy choices" at the bottom of any page reopens the same choice, and you can turn any group off again. When you switch something off we stop sending to it immediately and clear the cookies it set; the script that was already running in that tab is gone the next time the page loads. If we ever change what the groups mean, we'll ask you again rather than assume your old answer covers it.

Global Privacy Control

If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal — Brave, Firefox, DuckDuckGo and several extensions can — our code checks for it and, when present, turns off advertising and treats it as a "do not sell or share" request automatically, without asking you about it. We check for this signal on every visit, everywhere, not only in the handful of U.S. states that legally require honoring it at our size — that part is a choice we're making, not a legal obligation we're claiming, and we say so here so the promise stays accurate if it ever changes.

We don't sell or share data

We don't sell, rent, or trade any information you give us, to anyone, for any reason. Some privacy laws also count advertising measurement as "sharing", so the consent choice above includes a "do not sell or share my personal information" switch: turning it on switches advertising off and keeps it off, and nothing else you change afterwards will quietly undo it.

Deleting your information

You can delete a listing (and its photos) at any time from your dashboard. If you asked for someone's keepsake, the confirmation email you received has a link to withdraw that request and erase your note, email, and phone number — see "What a requester shares" above for the one thing that link can't undo (a chosen request whose contact info the giver already has by email). To remove your email or ask us to delete anything else we hold about you, write to hello@keepsake.ing and we'll take care of it.

What we keep and for how long: your device key and preferences live in your own browser's storage, not ours, until you clear them. Photos and listing text live for as long as the listing exists, plus a short grace period. Request notes/emails/phone numbers live until the request is withdrawn (see above) or you ask us to remove them. Rate-limit hashes and moderation records are kept only as long as they're useful for abuse prevention and the audit trail described elsewhere on this page.

Minimum age

Keepsake asks everyone posting or requesting a keepsake to confirm they're 18 or older — see Terms for why. We can't verify anyone's age; the point of asking is a documented, honest basis for removing an account the moment we learn someone using it is a minor.

Other services we rely on

Beyond what's named above (Cloudflare for hosting, storage, and AI screening; Google for the advertising tag, only after you consent), a few smaller services touch limited data on our behalf: Cloudflare Turnstile checks that a submission is coming from a real browser, not a bot, before it reaches our server; Sentry receives error reports if something breaks, so we can fix it, with obvious personal data redacted before it's sent; and Resend is a backup email provider we can fall back to if Cloudflare's own email sending isn't available. None of them receive your photos or story text beyond what's needed to run the service itself.

Questions
