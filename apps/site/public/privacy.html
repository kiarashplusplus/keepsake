Privacy

Last updated 7 September 2026 — see what changed

Keepsake doesn't have accounts, so there isn't much of you to collect in the first place. Here's exactly what we hold, and why.

Who runs this

Keepsake is run by one person, Kiarash Adl, operating as an individual — not through a company or any other entity. As of September 2026 that's a decision rather than a default: whether to put a company between him and this site was a question we actually sat down and answered, and the answer was no. He's the controller for the purposes of this policy: the one accountable for what's described here, and the one you'd contact, complain to, or send a rights request to. Reach him at hello@keepsake.ing — that inbox is read personally, not by a support team.

We don't publish a mailing address. That's a considered choice, not an omission: the UK is the one jurisdiction whose law would otherwise require one (UK GDPR Art. 13), and Keepsake doesn't serve the UK — see Terms. Canadian and U.S. privacy law, which do still apply, ask for an accountable person and a working way to reach them, which the paragraph above already gives you; none of it requires a physical address on top of that. If Keepsake ever expands somewhere that changes this, this section will change with it.

The point of not publishing one is that Keepsake is a person at a home address, and this is a site that arranges meetings between strangers and turns down listings — not that he's hard to reach. If you need a postal address for a legal notice, or because some form you're filling in demands one, ask at hello@keepsake.ing and you'll be given one.

No accounts, just a device key

When you post your first keepsake, your browser generates a private token and stores it in your device's local storage. That token is your identity on Keepsake — there's no username, password, or profile. If you clear your browser storage or switch devices without saving an email, we have no way to prove a listing is yours again.

An optional email

You can add an email address when you give something. We store it only to notify you when someone asks for your keepsake and to help you recover access to your dashboard if you lose your device key. We never use it for marketing, and it's never shown publicly on the site. You can take it back off at any time yourself, from keepsake.ing/mine/email — see "Your rights" below for what that does and how long we hold it if you don't.

It is shared privately, though, in one case: if you choose a request, we send your email address to that requester directly (in their notification, and as the reply-to address on it) so the two of you can arrange the handoff. This is the one moment either of your contact details becomes visible to the other person — see the next section.

What a requester shares

If you ask for someone's keepsake, we store the note, email, and optional phone number you provide with that request. This contact information is only shown to the giver, and only once they choose you — and if they do, your email is also included in the "you were chosen" notification we send you, the same way the giver's is (see above).

Every request-confirmation email includes a link to withdraw that request. Following it deletes your note, email, and phone number from our records and marks the request withdrawn — no account or login needed, since you never had one. If the giver already chose you before you withdraw, they may already have your contact information by email; withdrawing removes it from our database and the giver's dashboard going forward, but we can't recall an email that's already been delivered.

Photos

Photos you upload are stored on Cloudflare R2. We keep a full-size and a thumbnail version of each one for as long as the listing exists, plus 30 days after it's rejected, withdrawn, or hidden. If you upload a photo but never finish posting the listing, we delete it after 24 hours.

Location

A listing's location is a postcode centroid, not a device-precise coordinate. When you post something, we look up the latitude and longitude of the postcode you gave us in a fixed table built from public postal-code data — the same point every listing in that postcode gets, not a spot tied to your address or device. If you use "Use my location" while posting, your device's exact coordinates go straight from your browser to a reverse-geocoding lookup (BigDataCloud, or OpenStreetMap's Nominatim as a fallback) to work out your postcode. That exchange happens between your browser and those services directly — we only ever receive the postcode it hands back, never the coordinates.

Browsing "near me" is a separate feature, worth being precise about on its own: if you use "Use my location" to search rather than to post, your device's coordinates are sent to us as part of that one search request, so we can sort listings by distance — they aren't looked up from a postcode table, and we don't store them. Your browser remembers your last search location for next time, the same way it would a typed-in postcode, but our server only ever sees the coordinates for the moment it takes to answer that request.

Screening

Every listing's text and photos are checked automatically by AI models run by Cloudflare at the moment you post, to keep the marketplace safe. Some listings are also reviewed by a human — just Kiarash, today — before they go live. Keeping the marketplace safe is the only thing those models are used for; a different model reads your photo to help you fill in the form, which is the next section.

Autofill from your photo

Separately from screening, and for a completely different reason, the first photo you upload is also read by an AI vision model run by Cloudflare — to save you typing. It proposes a title for your keepsake, picks a category, writes one line about visible wear it can make out, suggests a handful of short tags (like "mug", "ceramic", "blue"), and returns a plain description of what it sees in the frame. This is a convenience and nothing more: it's a separate call from the safety screening above, and none of it decides whether your listing goes live.

What happens to the result. The title is a starting point — it's filled into the form for you, and anything you type over it wins. The category and the short tags are saved with your listing as ordinary listing content; the tags are how the browse page groups similar keepsakes together, so they're public in the same way your title and photo are. There's no category picker when you post, so the model's answer is the one that's saved — if it's wrong on your listing, write to us and we'll fix it. The line about visible wear and the description of what it sees never reach our records at all: the wear line is only offered as a prompt while you write your story, and both live in your browser's draft until you post or clear it. If you never finish the listing, none of it is kept — the photo itself goes after 24 hours, as above.

Rate limiting

To stop abuse, we keep a short-lived, hashed record of your IP address combined with the date (for example, sha256("2026-09-05:203.0.113.4")) instead of your raw IP address. We're being precise rather than absolute about what that buys you: the point is minimization, not secrecy. IPv4 addresses are a small enough space (a few billion) that someone who wanted to unmask one specific day's hash could brute-force it by hashing every possible address — this isn't a strong cryptographic guarantee against a motivated adversary. What it does do: we never handle or store your address in the clear at all, and the hash is scoped to a single day, so two days' records can't be joined into a history of your address the way a raw IP log could be. We keep each day's hash for 48 hours, then it expires automatically.

Two things use that hash: a brake that slows down a burst of requests within a single minute, and a counter for how much you've done today. Both use the same date-scoped hash, so neither one can follow you from one day into the next.

Your choice, before anything loads

The first time you arrive, Keepsake asks you one question, and until you answer it nothing optional runs. Rejecting everything is one click, in the same place and the same size as accepting. There are three groups:

Strictly necessary. Not a choice, because without it there is no site: the device key that proves a listing is yours, your light/dark preference, and the record of this very decision. All of it stays in your browser's local storage. None of it goes to anyone else.

Analytics. Off unless you say yes. If you say yes, we load Cloudflare Web Analytics from static.cloudflareinsights.com. It counts page views without cookies and without following you to other sites. Cloudflare is the recipient.

Advertising. Off unless you say yes. If you say yes, we load the Google Ads tag from www.googletagmanager.com so we can tell which advert brought you here. Google is the recipient, and it may set cookies on this site. We never send it your photos, your story text, your email, or anything a requester wrote to you.

One more thing worth saying plainly: if we haven't switched a tool on for a particular build, it doesn't load even when you've said yes. Consent is the ceiling, not the trigger.

Changing your mind

"Privacy choices" at the bottom of any page reopens the same choice, and you can turn any group off again. When you switch something off we stop sending to it immediately and clear the cookies it set; the script that was already running in that tab is gone the next time the page loads. If we ever change what the groups mean, we'll ask you again rather than assume your old answer covers it.

Global Privacy Control

If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control signal — Brave, Firefox, DuckDuckGo and several extensions can — our code checks for it and, when present, turns off advertising and treats it as a "do not sell or share" request automatically, without asking you about it. We check for this signal on every visit, everywhere, not only in the handful of U.S. states that legally require honoring it at our size — that part is a choice we're making, not a legal obligation we're claiming, and we say so here so the promise stays accurate if it ever changes.

We don't sell or share data

We don't sell, rent, or trade any information you give us, to anyone, for any reason. Some privacy laws also count advertising measurement as "sharing", so the consent choice above includes a "do not sell or share my personal information" switch: turning it on switches advertising off and keeps it off, and nothing else you change afterwards will quietly undo it.

Categories of information we collect

Gathered in one place, since some places ask for it that way: a device-generated identifier (your device key), an optional email address, an optional phone number (if you request something), a note you write when requesting a keepsake, and the title, story, photos, tags, category, and postcode-level location of a listing you post — the title, category and tags may be the ones a model read off your photo, see "Autofill from your photo" above. Separately, we keep a short-lived, hashed record of your IP address for abuse prevention (see "Rate limiting" above).

Two more categories reach us and are then thrown away rather than kept, which we list here anyway because they are still collected. The first is your date of birth: the age gate on the give form and the request form asks for it, it travels to us with that submission, it is checked once against our minimum age, and it is discarded — all we keep is a timestamp recording that the check passed (see "Minimum age" below). The second is your device's exact coordinates, but only if you use "Use my location" to search rather than to post: they come with that one search request and aren't stored (see "Location" above).

We don't sell any of it, to anyone, for any reason — see the section above. Kiarash, named under "Who runs this" above, is the controller for all of it.

Your rights: access, deletion, and portability

Because there's no account to log into, most of this is already self-service. You can delete a listing (and its photos) at any time from your dashboard. Your email address is self-service too: on the device that holds your key, open your dashboard and follow "Your email" to keepsake.ing/mine/email, where a Remove button erases the address from our records on the spot — you don't have to write to us and wait. If you asked for someone's keepsake, the confirmation email you received has a link to withdraw that request and erase your note, email, and phone number — see "What a requester shares" above for the one thing that link can't undo (a chosen request whose contact info the giver already has by email).

For anything that isn't self-service — seeing a copy of what we hold about you (access), getting it in a portable form, or removing anything on this page you can't remove yourself — write to hello@keepsake.ing and we'll take care of it. There's one operator and no account system behind this, so these are manual requests we handle by hand, not an automated export button, but we'll get you an answer.

What we keep and for how long: your device key, preferences, and last search location live in your own browser's storage, not ours, until you clear them. Photos and listing text live for as long as the listing exists, plus 30 days if it's rejected, withdrawn, or hidden. Request notes/emails/phone numbers live until the request is withdrawn (see above) or you ask us to remove them. A link we send to verify an email or recover your dashboard expires in 24 hours (or 30 minutes for a recovery link) and the record of it is cleared within a further week. Rate-limit hashes expire automatically after 48 hours (see "Rate limiting" above), and moderation and email-delivery logs are kept for 90 days as an abuse-prevention audit trail.

Your own email is the one thing on that list with no expiry date, and it's worth stating without hedging. If you add an email address, we keep it indefinitely, tied to your device key. There is no dormancy window, no inactivity timer, and no cleanup job that removes it: not opening Keepsake for a year, or five, deletes nothing. It is kept because it is the only way back to your listings if you lose your device key, and losing it silently to a timer would defeat the point of having offered it. It goes when you remove it — the Remove button described above, or by asking us — and not before.

Minimum age

You have to be 18 or older to post a keepsake or ask for one — see Terms for why. The form doesn't ask you to tick a box that states its own right answer. It asks a neutral question instead: your date of birth, typed as YYYY-MM-DD.

What happens to that date, exactly. It is sent to us along with the listing or the request — we're saying so plainly rather than implying it never leaves your browser — and there it is checked once, against nothing but our minimum age. Then it is dropped. No column in our database holds a date of birth and no code path writes it to one, copies it into a log, or passes it to anyone else. The only trace kept is a timestamp on that listing or request recording that the check passed on that day. On your side of the wire it is just as short-lived: unlike the rest of a draft listing, the field is never saved into your browser's storage, so it's gone when you post or leave the page.

None of this verifies anyone's age — a date you type is a self-declaration, not an age-verification system. Asking it neutrally is what gives us a documented, honest basis for acting the moment we actually learn — through a report, a listing, or a request — that someone using Keepsake is a minor. Acting on that means removing the listing or request involved, and, when it's a giver, banning the device key that posted it.

Other services we rely on

Beyond what's named above (Cloudflare for hosting, storage, AI screening, and the autofill model; Google for the advertising tag, only after you consent), a few smaller services touch limited data on our behalf: Cloudflare Turnstile checks that a submission is coming from a real browser, not a bot, before it reaches our server; and Resend is a backup email provider we can fall back to if Cloudflare's own email sending isn't available. If something breaks, error diagnostics stay inside Cloudflare — the same platform already hosting the app — rather than going to a separate error-tracking company, with obvious personal data redacted before anything is written. None of them receive your photos or story text beyond what's needed to run the service itself.
